How to share grassroots football results with parents

Practical advice for volunteer coaches and team managers: where to post results, what to share, and how to keep children safe while you do it.

By the Touchline Tales team · October 2026 · 6 minute read

Every Sunday, somebody has to tell the parents how it went. Usually that's the coach, typing on a phone in the car park with cold hands. Done well, it keeps families who couldn't make it involved, gives every child a moment to be proud of, and saves you answering the same question twelve times. Done carelessly, it can put children's details somewhere they shouldn't be.

Here's what we've learned about getting it right.

Group chat or a team page?

Most teams start with a WhatsApp or Messenger group, and for good reason: everyone already has it, and it's quick. It's the right place for the day-to-day: kick-off times, "pitch two, not pitch one", and who's bringing the oranges.

Group chats are less good as a record. Results sink under forty other messages, parents who join mid-season can't see what happened before, and screenshots get forwarded beyond the group without anyone meaning any harm.

A team page, whether a simple web page or a dedicated team site, is better for the things people want to look back on: results, line-ups, match reports and milestones. Grandparents and parents who work weekends can catch up when it suits them, and there's one link to share instead of a scroll through the chat.

Tip: use both. Keep the group chat for arrangements, and post one link to the result once it's up. That keeps the chat calm and the season in one place.

What to share after a match

Parents mostly want to know who played, how the team got on, and anything their child did that's worth a cheer. A good match update includes:

  • the opponent, date and competition (league, cup or friendly)
  • the squad, by first name
  • the result and goals, where your age group and club allow it (more on that below)
  • a few lines on the game: what went well, what the team is working on
  • any player of the match or effort award, with a reason

Keep reports positive and about the team. Over a season, try to mention every child, not just the goalscorers. And leave out injuries, medical details, behaviour issues, criticism of individual players and comments about referees or the other team.

Safeguarding basics

None of this needs to be complicated, but it does need to be done every time.

  • First names only. No surnames, ages, birthdays, schools or home areas. A first name, a team and a photo can be enough to identify a child, so be stricter than feels necessary.
  • Get photo consent first. Ask parents in writing before any photo of their child is shared, keep a note of who has said no, and check each picture for children whose parents haven't agreed. Never put a child's full name with their photo.
  • Mind the background. School badges, street signs and car number plates can say more than you meant to.
  • Talk to parents, not children. Match updates go to parents and carers. Don't message players directly.
  • Ask your welfare officer. Every club has a welfare officer who knows its photo and online policy and the FA's safeguarding guidance. Agree with them what goes public before the season starts.

Scores for Under 7s to Under 11s

The FA's youth football rules say that results and league tables aren't published for Under 7 to Under 11 matches. The idea is that young players should be learning the game and enjoying it, not worrying about a table. Children still know the score, of course, and that's fine.

For a coach, that means thinking about where you post. Many clubs ask their younger teams not to put scores or scorers anywhere public, and to celebrate effort, skills and team moments instead. Some are happy for scores to go in a private parents' group. Check what your club and league expect, and look at the current FA rules each season, as they're reviewed from time to time.

Keep children's details out of search results

Anything public on the web can end up in Google. A page that lists children's first names next to a club name and fixture times can be found by anyone searching for that club, not just the parents you meant it for.

  • If you use a team web page, make sure it's set to "noindex", which tells search engines not to list it. Whoever set the page up can add this in a minute.
  • Share the link through the club's own channels, such as the parents' group or the club newsletter, rather than posting it publicly.
  • Think twice before naming children in public social media posts, where club pages are usually open to everyone.

A simple matchday routine

  1. Before kick-off: post the squad and meeting time in the group chat.
  2. During the game: if parents are following from home, ask one parent on the touchline to post updates, so the coach can coach.
  3. Within a day: put up the result and a short report, then share one link in the chat.
  4. End of season: a round-up of the year, with something for every child.

All of this, built in
