A welfare officer's checklist for a team website

For club welfare officers and secretaries asked to sign off a website for one of your teams: what to check before any child's name goes on it.

By Murph Hartley, Touchline Tales · 25 October 2026 · 7 minute read

A coach comes to you with a website for their team. It looks great, the parents are keen, and the coach wants it live by Sunday. You're the one who has to say yes, and you're a volunteer too.

This checklist works for any team website or app, not just ours. Go through it with the coach, or with whoever is providing the site, and you'll know what you're agreeing to.

1. Who is responsible for the data?

When a team site holds children's first names, match records and availability, your club is usually the data controller: it decides what's collected and why. The company running the site is the processor, handling the data on the club's behalf.

  • Ask the provider for a data processing agreement and read it before the site goes live. It should say what data is held, where, for how long, and what happens if something goes wrong.
  • Check who the provider uses behind the scenes (hosting, database, email) and that they'll tell you before that changes.
  • Make sure the agreement says how quickly they'll tell you about a data breach.

2. What goes on the site

  • First names only. No surnames, ages, birthdays, schools or home areas. A first name with a team and a photo can be enough to identify a child.
  • Nothing sensitive. No medical notes, injuries, behaviour issues or family circumstances, even in coach-only areas, unless there's a clear reason and a lawful basis agreed with you.
  • Keep match reports kind. Reports should be about the team, with no criticism of individual children.

3. Photos and consent

  • Get each parent's agreement in writing before a photo of their child appears, and keep a note of who has said no.
  • Check that a parent can change their mind and have a photo taken down quickly.
  • Never put a child's full name with their photo, and watch the background for school badges, street signs and number plates.

4. Who can see it, and who can change it

  • Kept out of search engines. The site should carry a "noindex" tag, so Google and others don't list it. Ask the provider to confirm.
  • Shared through the club. The link goes to parents through the club's own channels, like the parents' group, not posted publicly.
  • Named editors only. Only the team's listed coaches should be able to change anything, each with their own sign-in, and no shared passwords. Ask how a coach is removed when they leave.
  • What parents can do. If parents can sign in, check what that lets them see and change, and that it's limited to their own child.

5. Scores for Under 7s to Under 11s

The FA's youth football rules say that results and league tables aren't published for Under 7 to Under 11 matches. If the site shows scores to visitors, check it can hide them for your younger teams, and agree with the coach what's shown. Check your league's own rules too, and the current FA rules each season, as they're reviewed from time to time.

6. Where the data is kept

  • Ask where the data is stored. In the UK is simplest; outside it, the agreement should explain the safeguards.
  • Check that private records, like availability and any parents' contact details, can't be read by visitors.
  • Check connections are encrypted (the address starts with https and shows a padlock).

7. When a team stops using it

  • Check the club can get a copy of its data when it leaves.
  • Check the provider deletes the site and its data afterwards, within a set time, and confirms it in writing.

The checklist on one page

  1. We have a data processing agreement, and the club has read it.
  2. Players are shown by first name only, with nothing sensitive.
  3. We have written photo consent, and a note of who said no.
  4. The site is kept out of search engines, and the link is shared through the club.
  5. Only named coaches can edit, each with their own sign-in.
  6. Scores are hidden for Under 7s to Under 11s, or the club has agreed otherwise.
  7. We know where the data is kept and that private records are locked.
  8. We know how to get our data back and have it deleted if we leave.

How Touchline Tales does it

Our answers to the checklist

Your club is the data controller and we're the processor, under our data processing agreement, which your club accepts before the site goes live. It promises to tell you about a breach within 48 hours and to give you 30 days' notice before we change who we use behind the scenes.

Players are shown by first name only. Team sites carry a noindex tag. Only listed coaches can change anything, each signing in with an email link.
