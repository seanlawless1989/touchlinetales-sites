Key takeaways

  • Under 7s football uses a size 3 ball, 5v5 format and a pitch of roughly 30 by 20 yards with 12x4 feet goals.
  • Plan the season in three ten-week phases: ball mastery, then 1v1 and 2v2, then small-sided matchcraft.
  • Keep every drill under two minutes with no queues longer than three or four players.
  • Run squads of eight to ten and rotate every child through every position, including goalkeeper.
  • A 60-minute weekly session: free dribbling arrival, one main drill, two small-sided games, free-play finish.

Coaching under 7s football works best when you plan the season in three phases: ball familiarity first, then 1v1 and 2v2 play, then small-sided matchcraft, all built on the FA's size 3 ball, 5v5 games and short pitches. This guide maps drills, pitch sizes and team structure to a 30-week plan you can run on one weeknight a week.

What size football should under 7s use?

Under 7s use a size 3 ball, which is the FA's requirement for 5v5 children's football. At roughly 20 inches around and around 350g, it fits small hands and feet, so players can actually strike it cleanly rather than shovelling a size 5 around the pitch.

Why a size 3 ball suits under 7s

A size 5, the adult ball, is too heavy for a six or seven year old to lift over a short distance, which kills first-touch work and shooting technique. The FA's youth format guidance sets the size 3 alongside small pitches and 5v5 for exactly this reason (The FA children's football formats). Buy three or four so nobody queues at training.

Getting the inflation right

Pump a size 3 to around 0.6 bar (9 psi), firm enough to bounce off the foot, soft enough to cushion a heavy first touch. Check weekly, because a ball left in a car boot all week loses pressure fast.

How big should the pitch and goals be for under 7s football?

Under 7s football is played 5v5, on a pitch roughly 30 by 20 yards with goals 12 feet wide and 4 feet high. That is a fraction of a full-size pitch, because small-sided games mean more touches, more shots and fewer children standing still.

Marking out a pitch on a school field

You rarely get a marked mini-pitch, so cone one out on the school field or local rec: 30 paces long, 20 paces wide, with a halfway line and a centre circle of about 4 yards. Portable 12x4 goals or a pair of corner poles with a rope work fine if your club has no goals. Once the pitch is set, keep the layout identical every week, and share matchday results and photos with parents afterwards so nobody has to reconstruct the morning from the group chat.

How many players should be in an under 7s football team?

Squad size vs matchday numbers

Under 7s play 5v5, including the goalkeeper. The FA's youth football guidance recommends a squad of roughly eight, so every child gets real playing time even with absences on a Sunday morning. Keep your registered squad at eight to ten; more than that and you'll be explaining to a five-year-old why they didn't get on.

Rotating positions without chaos

Rotate everyone through every position across the season, including goalkeeper, so no child gets parked in defence all year. A simple trick: swap outfield positions at each quarter break and announce it before the restart so nobody stands confused. The FA's ethos for this age is no results recording and no published tables, so you can experiment freely (FA youth football guidance).

Fair playing time is also a welfare duty, not just good manners. If a parent raises it, our welfare officer checklist covers the conversation.

What are the best football drills for under 7s?

The best drills for under 7s are short games with a ball each, grouped by skill: ball familiarity first, then dribbling and 1v1, then passing and shooting. Anything with queues longer than three or four players loses them.

Ball familiarity drills

Every child needs a ball at their feet from the first minute. Toe taps, sole rolls and stop-start dribbling around their own space take no explaining and give every player hundreds of touches. Follow-the-leader works well: one child dribbles, everyone copies. Keep each game to three or four minutes, then change it.

Dribbling and 1v1 drills

Dribbling is the whole game at this age, so give it the most time. Sharks and minnows, traffic lights and gates dribbling all reward keeping the ball close while changing speed and direction. Move quickly from dribbling games to 1v1: a player dribbles through a gate, defender tries to win it, attacker scores by dribbling through. 1v1 is where you see whether the touches stuck, and it is the closest thing to a real match they will get in training.

Rotate pairs every two minutes so nobody gets stuck defending the same strong player all session. If a child wins their 1v1, say so out loud; it is the cheapest confidence boost you have. If you want a record of who is winning theirs across the season so you can balance playing time, a tool like Touchline Tales (https://touchlinetales.club/dpa/) keeps that per player.

Passing and shooting drills

Passing comes third because most under 7s are not physically ready for it, but a simple pair passing through cones is worth ten minutes. Shooting: everyone lines up, one ball each, hit it, fetch it. Keep queues short.

Drills to avoid at this age

Skip anything with long queues, static line drills, headed headers, and complex set pieces. Standing still is the enemy: if more than two children are waiting, change the drill.

How do you structure a first training session for an under 7s team?

Keep the first session simple: short games, no long explanations, and finish with free play so they leave wanting more. Before anyone arrives, make sure you meet the FA's safeguarding basics for coaching young children, including the relevant checks and safeguarding children training described on the FA's safeguarding pages.

The 60-minute session template

  • 0–5 min: Arrival, free dribbling with a ball each. No talking yet.
  • 5–10 min: Warm-up game, like sharks and minnows, where everyone has a ball.
  • 10–25 min: One dribbling drill, e.g. traffic lights (green = go, red = stop, amber = toe taps). Explain in under 30 seconds.
  • 25–40 min: A small-sided game, 3v3 or 4v4, with two balls if numbers allow.
  • 40–55 min: Second game with a new rule, e.g. a goal only counts after three passes.
  • 55–60 min: Free play, then gather for one sentence of praise and a player of the match shout-out.

What to do when half the squad turns up

It happens at under 7s. Drop to 2v2 or 3v3, run both balls, and let the drills shrink rather than cutting games. Small numbers mean more touches per child, which is the whole point at this age.

How do you keep under 7s engaged during drills?

Under 7s give you about two minutes of real attention per activity. Plan for that, not against it.

The 90-second rule for drill changes

Change the drill every 90 seconds to two minutes, whether or not everyone has "finished". Standing still is where trouble starts, so run drills with no queues: split the squad into small grids of three or four, use a ball per pair where you can, and set the next activity up while the current one runs. If you are explaining for longer than they are moving, cut your explanation in half and show it instead.

Turning drills into games

Wrap everything in a story. Sharks and minnows beats "reaction dribbling", treasure hunters beats "shuttle runs", and scoring against a "castle" of cones beats shooting practice. Add a countdown, a villain, or a points race and the same drill runs twice as long. Keep the games short, keep everyone playing, and end each one on a cheer rather than a queue.

When can under 7s join a football league and what are the match rules?

League entry and festivals

Most county FAs let children join competitive football at Under 7, but many clubs start with festivals and friendly fixtures instead, where no scores count. Ask your club secretary or welfare officer what your league offers, since formats vary by county. Either way, matches are small-sided and short, not a mini Premier League.

Match rules that differ from 11v11

Under 7s play 5v5 on a small pitch with size 3 balls, quartered playing time, and a retreat line so the opposition drops back at goal kicks, giving your keeper a fair chance to play out. Throw-ins, kick-offs and free kicks all follow simplified versions of the adult laws, and the FA's youth adaptations to Law 1 (the pitch) and Law 2 (the ball) are set out in the FA's rules and laws guidance.

Recording the season without league tables

Results and tables are not published at this age, so the season's record lives with you: who played, who scored a first goal, who was player of the match. A team site in your club's colours keeps that history in one place, instead of it dissolving into the group chat.

A week-by-week season plan for under 7s football drills

Thirty weeks splits neatly into three blocks of ten, each building on the last. Repeat the same session shape every week: a ball-everyone arrival game, one main drill, one small-sided finish. Familiar structure means less explaining and more playing.

Phase 1: weeks 1-10, ball mastery

Every child has a ball for most of the session. Toe taps, sole rolls, dribbling through cones, stopping the ball dead on a signal. Weeks 8-10 add changes of direction at walking pace. The only aim is comfort: nobody looks down at the ball by week 10.

Phase 2: weeks 11-20, 1v1 and 2v2

Introduce an opponent. Weeks 11-15 run 1v1 gates: attacker tries to dribble through a gate, defender tries to win the ball. Weeks 16-20 move to 2v2, where the first pass appears naturally because a teammate is now the better option. Defenders learn to jockey rather than dive in.

Phase 3: weeks 21-30, small-sided matchcraft

Finish with 3v3 and 4v4 games on a small pitch, with the season's match rules in force. Use the games to coach one theme per week: spread out, pass when someone's closer to goal, celebrate the first goal and the player of the match like everything else. Keep playing time equal; fair rotation matters more than results at this age.

Log each week's theme and who played where in one place, so by the spring presentation you can show every child their own season, not a lost WhatsApp scroll.

FAQ

How long should an under 7s training session last?

Sixty minutes, run once a week on a weeknight. Fill the hour with a free dribbling arrival game, a warm-up, one main drill, two small-sided games and a short free-play finish. Attention runs out before the hour does, so end on a cheer while they still want more.

Do under 7s football matches have goalkeepers?

Yes. Under 7s play 5v5 and one player on each side is the goalkeeper, so a squad of eight gives you cover. Rotate every child through the position across the season rather than parking your best handler in goal all year. The retreat line at goal kicks gives your keeper a fair chance to play out.

What qualifications do I need to coach an under 7s football team?

Clubs and leagues expect you to meet the FA's safeguarding basics before taking a session: the relevant criminal record checks and safeguarding children training. Formal coaching badges are not required at this age. Ask your club secretary which checks they will run for you, since most handle the paperwork.

How do you teach under 7s to pass instead of swarming the ball?

Mostly wait, and design for it. Most under 7s are not physically ready to pass, which is why passing sits third in the season plan after ball mastery and 1v1 work. Swarming fades once 2v2 games in weeks 16-20 make a teammate the better option. A rule like a goal only counting after three passes nudges the habit along.
