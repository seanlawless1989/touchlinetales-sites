A junior football team kit bundle costs roughly £20-£35 per player for shirt, shorts and socks, depending on whether you go adidas or a generic brand. This guide gives you an age-by-age sizing worksheet, a cost-per-player breakdown, and a treasurer's checklist so you can settle the whole order in one evening.

How much should a junior football team kit bundle cost per player?

Budget £25–£40 per player for a workable bundle at Under 7s to Under 11s, and £40–£60 once you add a second shirt for older age groups. The three things that move the price are shirt quality (polyester weight and printing), whether you buy a full kit or just shirts, and how many spare shirts you hold for growth spurts and new signings.

What a bundle typically includes

A sensible bundle is: home shirt with squad numbers, shorts, socks, and a spare shirt or two per squad. Goalkeeper kit is usually separate and often shared across squads to spread the cost. The FA's guidance on youth football covers kit and safeguarding expectations your supplier should meet, so check the FA's grassroots pages before you commit.

What should be included in a junior football team kit bundle?

The core eleven items

Start with the essentials: a shirt, shorts and socks per player, plus a goalkeeper kit in a clashing colour. Add a numbered set (1 to 16 covers most squads) so you are not reassigning shirts every Sunday. Then the coach kit: a bib set or training vests, a first aid bag, and a ball bag. That is the bundle. Everything else is negotiable.

Nice-to-haves versus money-wasters

Rain jackets and track tops look smart on the touchline but double your per-player cost, and most Under 7s to Under 11s will outgrow them in a season. Skip embroidered crests on training gear and match-day pennants entirely. If your budget is tight, spend it on spare shirts for growth spurts rather than extras.

One genuinely useful add-on costs nothing: photograph the line-up and results each week and share them properly, so the season lives beyond the group chat. We cover how to do that in our guide to sharing results with parents.

How do you size junior football kits across different age groups?

Age-to-size mapping for Under 7s to Under 16s

Youth kit sizes roughly follow age bands, but chest measurement is the number that matters. adidas publishes a size chart mapping youth ages to chest and waist measurements, so an age 7-8 years size fits a chest around 60-65cm and age 12-13 years around 78-83cm (adidas size charts). An Under 7s squad will mostly sit in one size, while an Under 12s squad will span three.

Measuring players without a fitting night

You will not get 14 children in a room on a Tuesday, so measure at training instead. Ask a parent to measure chest circumference under the arms with a soft tape, then text you the number. If you cannot measure everyone, order by age but size up for any child visibly near the top of their band. A loose kit lasts a season; a tight one lasts a term.

Keep a note of each player's size in one place alongside your squad records, the same way you would track player details on your team site or in your welfare paperwork (https://touchlinetales.club/guides/welfare-officer-checklist/). This is the step most volunteer coaches skip, and it is the reason a third of the bundle ends up unwearable by February: sizes get guessed from memory, the WhatsApp messages asking "what size is Tommy?" go unanswered, and the spare box becomes the de facto fitting room every Sunday. Thirty minutes of tape-measure work before you order saves a re-order, which is where kits actually get expensive.

Ordering spares for growth spurts

Add one spare shirt and one pair of shorts per two or three players, sized at the top of your squad's range. Under 9s to Under 12s grow fastest, so weight your spares there. If a player outgrows their kit mid-season, the spare covers matchday while you order a replacement individually.

How do adidas junior football team kits compare to generic alternatives?

What the brand premium buys you

Branded ranges like adidas buy consistency: the same shirt, shorts and socks cut is available year after year, so a replacement for a growth spurt matches the rest of the squad. You also get the junior sizing charts and the option of a club crest applied at the factory. That matters most for Under 7s to Under 11s, where a shirt that shrinks two sizes mid-season is a real problem.

Where generic kits win

Generic suppliers usually cost less per player and ship faster on small orders, which suits a volunteer coach ordering for one team with a tight budget. If your squad is older, roughly the same size, and unlikely to need replacements, a generic bundle does the job. Fair playing time means spare shirts get heavy rotation anyway, so branding wears out fast.

A like-for-like comparison framework

Judge both on four things: price per player, size availability across the age range, replacement lead time, and whether the crest and sponsor can be added. Apply the same test to whichever supplier you shortlist, and be honest where your own club's setup falls short. How do you order kits for a whole junior football team in one go?

Building the squad size list

Start with a squad list, not a guess. Ask every parent for shirt size, shorts size and squad number preference in one message, then chase the two families who never reply. Keep the list somewhere you control rather than buried in the group chat; if you store children's names and sizes on a team site like Touchline Tales, check its data terms first at https://touchlinetales.club/dpa/.

Lead times, minimums and reorders

Order for your largest realistic squad, not your current headcount: most junior squads grow by January. Bulk team kit ranges carry minimum order quantities, and personalised printing (names, numbers, sponsor) adds one to two weeks. Check current junior team prices on the official range at adidas UK before committing, and confirm the reorder path for a lost or outgrown shirt mid-season.

Sponsorship and printing considerations

Get sponsor artwork as a print-ready vector file before you order, not a WhatsApp photo. Agree with your welfare officer what goes on a shirt worn by Under 7s, and put the sponsor's money towards spare shirts rather than fancier ones.

What should parents check before a club commits to a kit supplier?

Returns, samples and child-safety basics

Ask whether the supplier will send a sample shirt in two or three sizes before you commit to a full squad order, and who pays the postage if it doesn't fit. Confirm the returns policy in writing: printed names and numbers usually can't be returned, so a sizing mistake lands on the club. Check that shirts carry no loose drawcords around the neck, which FA guidance flags for junior players, and ask where the kit is actually made if a parent raises it.

Payment and data questions for the treasurer

Get the total cost per player, including printing, in one figure before you collect money from parents. Ask whether the supplier takes card payments with an invoice you can file for the club accounts, and whether they store children's names or photos on their systems, and for how long. If you're using sponsor money, confirm the sponsor's logo placement is agreed before printing.

How do you budget for replacements and mid-season growth spurts?

Kids grow. An Under 9s shirt that fits in September can be tight by March, so plan for two kinds of replacement: swapped shirts mid-season and full re-orders next year.

A simple contingency rule

Hold 10-15% of your kit budget as cash, plus one spare shirt and one spare pair of shorts per age group in the kit bag. Price the spares against current listings rather than last season's invoice, since UK suppliers such as Teamzum publish junior bundle prices that move season to season. If a player outgrows a shirt, the spare covers matchdays while you order a single replacement instead of a new bundle.

Tracking kit and player records without spreadsheet chaos

A shared spreadsheet dies by October because nobody updates it after Sunday. Keep it simple: one row per player, columns for shirt size, shirt number, and whether kit has been handed out or returned. Treasurer's decision checklist: choosing your junior football team kit

Before you get quotes

  • Count the squad plus two spare shirts per age group, and note how many players will outgrow the kit by Christmas.
  • Set a per-player budget and get committee sign-off before approaching suppliers.
  • Agree who owns the club shop login, and whether parents order their own or you place one bulk order.
  • Check whether your league mandates a specific sleeve colour or number style.

Before you click order

  • Get a sample shirt in the smallest and largest age-group sizes and have two players try them on.
  • Confirm the price includes numbering, crest printing and delivery, not just the shirt.
  • Ask the supplier what the turnaround is from payment to delivery, in writing.
  • Order two extra shirts in the next size up for growth spurts, and keep the invoice for next season's reorder.

FAQ

How do I join a junior football team if my child has never played before?

Contact local clubs directly; most grassroots sides accept beginners at any age and hold open sessions where your child can try a few weeks before you commit. Ask the club secretary what kit is provided and what you pay for. Registration usually runs through the club, and the FA's grassroots pages explain the process.

Do Argentina and Brazil junior football team shirts count as club kits for grassroots use?

No. International shirts are fan replicas, not club kits, and leagues require a matching team kit with squad numbers in your club's registered colours. A replica Argentina shirt also lacks the durability of team-grade polyester. Buy a proper bundle for matchdays and treat international shirts as casual wear or presents instead.

How many spare shirts should I order for an Under 8s squad?

One or two spare shirts is enough for an Under 8s squad of ten or so, sized at the top of your range. Growth spurts hit hardest from Under 9s upwards, so Under 8s need fewer. A spare covers a new signing or a mid-season outgrowth while you order a single replacement.

Can I put children's names on the back of junior football team kits?

Technically yes, but most clubs skip it. Printed names add cost, extend lead times by one to two weeks, and usually make shirts non-returnable, so a sizing mistake lands on the club. Squad numbers alone are cheaper and let you reassign shirts between players. Check your league's rules before committing either way.

What's the difference between a junior football team kit and a junior football kits team deal?

A team kit is the set of shirts, shorts and socks for one squad. A team deal bundles that with extras such as numbering, a goalkeeper shirt, a bib set or a ball bag, usually at a lower per-player price than buying separately. Check what the deal actually includes, since some pad the headline with items you would skip.
