What are the FA mini soccer rules for 2025-26?

Mini soccer is the FA's modified version of football for younger children, played as 5v5 or 7v7 on smaller pitches with smaller goals and no offside. The rules apply to teams from Under 7 up to Under 10, with Under 11s usually stepping up to 9v9 the following season.

Where the official FA rules live

Everything sits in one document: the FA mini soccer and youth football laws handbook, published on the FA's own site each season. If a league or county FA hands you a different version, ask them to confirm it matches the current handbook.

What changed for the 2025-26 season

The core laws are stable year to year; the practical changes coaches notice are usually in how their league applies them, such as kick-in versus throw-in at certain ages. Check your league's handbook alongside the FA's.

Which age groups play mini soccer

Under 7s and Under 8s play 5v5, Under 9s and Under 10s play 7v7. Whether you keep records in a spreadsheet or a team site like Touchline Tales, the format your team plays is fixed by age, not by choice.

What's the difference between 5v5 and 7v7 mini soccer rules?

The FA's mini soccer rules split by age: Under 7s and Under 8s play 5v5, Under 9s and Under 10s play 7v7, and both formats use smaller pitches, roll-on roll-off substitutions, and restarts designed to keep more touches per child. The details that catch coaches out are the retreat line, goal size, and maximum playing time, because they change between the two formats.

Squad size and substitutions

At 5v5 you name a squad of up to 10 players; at 7v7 it's up to 12. Substitutions are rolling in both, so any child who has been substituted can come back on, and every player who turns up should get game time. Keep a simple record of minutes per player each week, because your welfare officer may be asked how playing time was shared, and the welfare officer checklist covers what to have ready.

How do kick-ins work under FA mini soccer rules?

At Under 7s to Under 9s, the ball crossing the touchline is restarted with a kick-in rather than a throw-in. The FA made the change because young children struggle to throw a ball overhead with correct technique, and a kick keeps the game moving while giving them more touches. A kick-in is awarded to the team that did not touch the ball last, taken from where it went out, with the ball stationary and the kicker not playing it again until another player touches it. The FA's youth football law changes for 2025-26 confirm the format continues this season, so check your county FA too in case it adds local guidance.

How opponents must retreat and stand

Opponents must stand at least 4 metres away from the ball until it is in play. If they crowd the kick, the referee makes them retreat before it is taken.

Common kick-in mistakes referees see

Coaches see three every Sunday: an opponent standing closer than 4 metres, a player tapping the ball and dribbling straight away, and a kick-in taken from the wrong spot. What are the retreat line and goalkeeper rules in mini soccer?

How the retreat line works

The retreat line asks opposing players to move back to a marked line (roughly a third of the pitch from the goal) whenever the goalkeeper has the ball in their hands or takes a goal kick. Opponents cannot cross that line until the ball is in play. At 5v5 and 7v7 the principle is the same; the line simply sits proportionally further up the pitch as teams get bigger.

Goalkeeper restart and distribution rules

Once opponents have retreated, the goalkeeper can roll, throw or kick the ball to a teammate, or play it out while opponents wait behind the line. There is no restriction on the goalkeeper using their feet within their own half, so a keeper can dribble or pass under pressure once the ball is live. The point is to give young keepers a calm first touch on the ball, not to freeze play.

Why the FA introduced these rules

The FA wants keepers and defenders to build play from the back instead of hoofing it, which develops ball skills early. What pitch and goal sizes do the FA rules require for mini soccer?

Pitch and goal dimensions by format

The FA's standard codes set 5v5 on pitches of roughly 30-40 yards by 20-30 yards with 12ft by 4ft goals, and 7v7 on pitches of 50-60 yards by 30-40 yards with 12ft by 6ft goals. Your league's handbook will confirm the exact measurements it has adopted, and most venues mark to the middle of the permitted range. If your ground cannot fit a full 7v7 pitch, speak to your league before the season starts: many accept reduced dimensions for younger age groups. County FAs publish guidance on local variations, such as Kent FA's grassroots support pages, so check yours rather than guessing.

Ball size by age group

Under 7s and Under 8s use a size 3 ball; Under 9s and Under 10s move to size 4. Keep a spare of each in the kit bag, because matchday referees will check. We log any venue or equipment changes agreed with the league on our changelog so parents see the same version of the rules you do.

How long can matches be under FA mini soccer rules?

Maximum match lengths by age group

At Under 7s and Under 8s (5v5), halves run up to 10 minutes. Under 9s and Under 10s (7v7) play halves of up to 12½ minutes, and Under 11s (9v9) play halves of up to 12½ minutes at 9v9 festivals or up to 15 minutes where the format applies. Across all mini soccer age groups, a child should play no more than 60 minutes of football in one day, so a double-header or festival needs planning around that ceiling.

Playing time and fair rotation

The FA expects every squad member to get meaningful minutes, and at Under 7s to Under 11s rolling substitutions are standard, so you can change players at any stoppage without a formal process. In practice that means rotating so nobody spends three weeks on the bench, and tracking who has played what across the season. A simple note after each match, kept anywhere from a spreadsheet to your team site, makes fair playing time defensible when a parent asks.

Do league and county rules ever differ from the FA mini soccer rules?

Yes. The FA sets the national framework for mini soccer, but youth leagues can apply variations, and county FAs sometimes issue their own guidance on top. Common local differences include how kick-ins are taken, whether the retreat line is marked or enforced, and how strictly maximum playing time is policed. Spectator behaviour rules also vary: some leagues require a marked respect line or barriers behind the touchline, and the FA's safeguarding guidance covers what clubs are expected to provide for the touchline area (FA safeguarding guidance).

How to confirm your league's variations

Check three places before the first fixture: your league's handbook or website, your county FA's youth rules page, and the pre-season managers' meeting notes. If the handbook is silent, email the league secretary and get the answer in writing. Never assume the national rules apply unchanged to your fixture.

How do you keep parents up to date with the rules each season?

What to tell parents before the first match

Send one short pre-season note covering what changes this year: kick-ins instead of throw-ins for younger age groups, the retreat line, and how long each half runs. Ask parents to read it once rather than relaying it in the group chat. Pin the same summary wherever your team communicates so late joiners can find it.

FAQ

Can my Under 8s head the ball in mini soccer?

No. The FA bans deliberate heading in matches and training for Under 7s and Under 8s, with graduated limits through older age groups. If a ball strikes a child's head, play restarts with an indirect free kick to the opposing team. Build heading technique gradually from the age guidance your county FA publishes, using lightweight balls in training.

Are offside and penalties part of mini soccer?

No offside in either format, which is why the retreat line exists to stop children camping next to the goalkeeper. Penalties are awarded for direct free kick offences inside the penalty area, taken from the relevant penalty mark. Most fouls elsewhere result in an indirect free kick, so explain the difference to your players before the first match.

What happens if a kick-in is taken incorrectly?

The referee usually has the kick retaken rather than handing possession over, since mini soccer prioritises learning over punishment. Common faults include the ball moving before the kick, the taker playing it twice, or standing in the wrong place. Opponents within 4 metres should also be moved back. Expect a patient whistle, not a strict one.

Do parents and spectators have to stand behind a line at mini soccer matches?

Often, yes. Many leagues require a marked respect line or physical barrier behind the touchline, and enforcement varies by league. Spectators should also stay clear of the retreat line and goal areas so children can play without adults shouting over their shoulders. Check your league handbook before the season, because expectations differ between competitions.

My league plays 9v9 at Under 11 — do these rules still apply?

Partly. Under 11s typically step up to 9v9, which sits outside the 5v5 and 7v7 formats in this guide, though principles such as rolling substitutions and the 60-minute daily playing ceiling carry over. Pitch and goal sizes change again at 9v9, so treat this guide as covering Under 7s to Under 10s and read your league's Under 11 rules separately.
