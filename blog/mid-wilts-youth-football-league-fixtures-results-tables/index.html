The Mid Wilts Youth Football League runs grassroots youth football for teams across Wiltshire, publishing fixtures, results and tables online each season. This guide shows how the league is structured by age group and division, and exactly where to find live fixtures and results for your team every Sunday.

What is the Mid Wilts Youth Football League and who plays in it?

The Mid Wilts Youth Football League is a grassroots football league for youth teams based in and around mid Wiltshire, sitting alongside other Wiltshire leagues in the county's youth setup. It runs sanctioned Saturday and Sunday competition for clubs affiliated through their local County FA, with fixtures, results and tables published online each season so coaches and touchline parents can follow every round.

Which clubs and age groups take part

Member clubs field teams across the youth age groups, from the youngest mini-soccer sections up through 9v9 and 11v11 as players move toward open age football. Like most county youth leagues, it publishes its fixtures and results through the FA's Full-Time system, the same results platform many leagues use. If you want a simple way to share each week's scoreline with parents once you've checked it there, our guide covers sharing results with parents.

What age groups and divisions does the Mid Wilts Youth Football League run?

The league organises teams by age band, starting at Under 7s and running upwards through the youth age groups. Each band follows FA youth football guidance, so formats and pitch sizes change as children get older.

How divisions are set at the start of the season

Within each age group, teams are placed into divisions so clubs of similar strength play each other. Clubs submit entries over the summer, the league sorts teams into divisions based on prior results and known squads, and fixtures follow. Where an age group is small, it may run as a single division; larger age groups split into two or more.

Where mini-soccer rules change the format

The youngest bands play mini-soccer, which means small-sided games, no published league tables in the earliest years, and a focus on fair playing time rather than results. As a rough guide (illustrative, check current FA rules):

Where can you find Mid Wilts Youth Football League fixtures and results online?

Step 1: find the league's official site

Search for "Mid Wilts Youth Football League" and look for the league's own domain rather than a results aggregator. The league is sanctioned through the county body, so the Wiltshire FA lists affiliated youth leagues and can confirm you have the right site if you are unsure.

Step 2: use the fixtures and results pages

Most county youth leagues, Mid Wilts included, publish a fixtures page, a results page and a set of division tables, usually split by age group and then by division within that age group. Fixtures for the coming Sunday typically go up midweek once the league has confirmed pitches and referees. Results appear over Sunday evening and Monday as team managers phone or upload them in, so do not panic if the 10:00 kick-off you watched is still showing blank at teatime.

Step 3: filter to your team and bookmark it

The fastest route is to filter the full fixture list down to your own team, then bookmark that filtered URL. On most league sites the filter is a dropdown for age group plus a second one for division or team name; once both are set, the address in your browser bar is your team's season page. Save it on your phone and share it in the group chat rather than re-answering "what time are we kicking off?" every Friday. The same bookmark shows postponements and rearranged dates as the league updates them, which saves you relaying second-hand news from another manager. If you also want a record of results and player of the match across the season rather than just this week's fixture, a team site like Touchline Tales keeps that history in one place alongside the league's official listings.

How does the Mid Wilts league compare to nearby leagues like Testway and Bournemouth?

If your team crosses over into a neighbouring league for a cup run, a tournament or a move of club, the fixture and result setup will feel familiar but not identical. All three publish fixtures, results and tables online, so the habit you build checking Mid Wilts each week transfers directly.

Testway Youth Football League: where to look

Testway covers youth football around the Test Valley and neighbouring parts of Hampshire. Like Mid Wilts, its fixtures, results and division tables live on the league's own website, organised by age group and division. If you are used to finding your Under 10s' Sunday kick-off time on the Mid Wilts site, you will find the same information in the same shape on the Testway site: pick your age group, pick your division, read the fixture list.

Bournemouth Youth Football League: where to look

The Bournemouth youth league runs a larger operation on the south coast, but the principle holds: fixtures, results and tables are published online by age group and division. The practical difference for a visiting coach is scale, not structure. Expect more divisions per age group, so check you are looking at the right division before you confirm a kick-off time or venue with your parents.

Which other county youth leagues publish results in a similar way?

If you coach outside Mid Wilts, the pattern is much the same: most county youth leagues run a Full-Time or league-specific site where fixtures, results and tables sit a click or two from the homepage. Brentwood Youth Football League, Merseyside Youth Alliance, Stourbridge and District, Cheltenham Youth League and Harrow Youth League all follow this model, with age groups mapped to the FA's formats: 5v5 mini-soccer at the youngest ages, 9v9 in the middle years, then 11v11. The FA's guidance on youth football formats explains those age-band rules, which is why you will see tables split the way they do.

Snoop (Surrey Youth League, often nicknamed by its old branding) behaves similarly. The practical difference between leagues is rarely the setup; it is how quickly results get entered and whether postponements are flagged clearly.

How are match postponements and rearranged fixtures communicated in youth leagues like Mid Wilts?

Postponements usually follow the same chain in grassroots leagues: the league or a club official confirms a cancellation, the message reaches the club secretary, the secretary passes it to each team manager, and the manager relays it to parents. Rearranged fixtures work the same way in reverse, with the new date flowing back down the chain once the league fixes it.

Who tells parents, and when

Parents hear about it last, often hours before kick-off, because each handoff adds delay. A Saturday morning pitch inspection can mean a WhatsApp message at 8am and half a team already on the road. The weak link is rarely the league: it is the volunteer manager re-typing the news into the group chat while packing the cones.

A team site helps here. Publishing the cancellation and new date in one place, alongside the fixtures list, means parents check once instead of scrolling a thread.

What do clubs need to do to register a team in the Mid Wilts Youth Football League?

Registration is handled by the club secretary, not the coach, and it happens in three stages. First, the club affiliates with the county FA, which covers insurance and safeguarding checks for everyone on the touchline. Second, each player is registered with proof of age, usually a photo and a birth document, so they are eligible from the first fixture. Third, the club submits its team entry by the league's pre-season deadline, typically in early summer before fixtures are published.

Neighbouring leagues follow the same pattern. The Testway Youth Football League publishes its own entry and affiliation details on its site, which is a useful reference if your club plays across both leagues.

The practical takeaway: get your welfare officer's paperwork moving early, because player registrations cannot finish without it.

How do parents follow their child's team across a season in the Mid Wilts league?

Why the group chat is not a season record

The WhatsApp group is brilliant at telling you kick-off is 10:30 on Sunday and hopeless at telling you what happened in September. Messages bury themselves within hours, and by March nobody can find the line-up from the cup tie or who scored the first goal for the Under 8s.

Three habits fix this. First, bookmark the Mid Wilts Youth Football League fixtures page and check it before every matchday, because postponements and rearranged games land there first. Second, follow your team's own site if the coach keeps one; a page in the club's crest and colours that lists every fixture, result and player of the match gives you something the group chat never will. Third, keep your own season note: one line per match, your child's minutes and milestones.

By May you have a record worth keeping, and the coach has one less Sunday night job.

FAQ

Is the Mid Wilts Youth Football League part of the FA and sanctioned by Wiltshire FA?

The league runs sanctioned competition for clubs affiliated through their local County FA, and Wiltshire FA lists affiliated youth leagues, so you can confirm the league's status there. That sanctioning covers insurance and safeguarding checks for everyone involved, and it is why the league's fixtures and results appear on the FA's Full-Time system each season.

What happens if my child's fixture is called off on a Saturday morning?

The cancellation travels down a chain: league or club official, club secretary, team manager, then parents. Each handoff adds delay, so a Saturday pitch inspection might mean a WhatsApp message at 8am with half the team already travelling. Check the league's fixtures page first, since postponements and new dates land there before the group chat catches up.

Can I look up last season's results and tables for the Mid Wilts league?

Results and tables appear on the league's own site and on Full-Time, but historic archives vary by league and are not guaranteed to stay online year to year. If you want a lasting season record for your child's squad, keep your own: bookmark the filtered fixtures page, and keep one line per match covering minutes, milestones and who scored.

Do Mid Wilts fixtures change week to week, or are they published for the whole season?

Divisions are set before the season starts, but fixtures for the coming Sunday typically go up midweek once pitches and referees are confirmed. Postponements and rearranged dates then shift individual matches as the season goes on. The filtered fixtures page shows those changes first, so bookmark it and check before every matchday rather than relying on an old printout.

How do I find my team's league table on a phone at the touchline?

Filter the league's fixture list by age group, then by division or team name using the dropdowns on the site. Once both are set, the address in your browser bar is your team's season page. Bookmark that filtered URL on your phone and share it in the group chat, so nobody asks where the table is each Sunday.
