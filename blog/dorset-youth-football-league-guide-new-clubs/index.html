The Dorset Youth Football League runs age-group divisions from the youngest mini-soccer sections through to youth football, and getting a new or newly promoted team into it comes down to three things: registering your club and players inside the summer window, meeting the FA safeguarding checks, and keeping on top of fixture admin once the season starts. This guide walks you through each step, written for the volunteer coach doing it for the first time.

What age groups and divisions does the Dorset Youth Football League run?

The league covers the full youth pathway, from Under 7 mini-soccer right through to open-age youth football, with formats changing as children get older. Smaller pitches and fewer players early on, full 11v11 by the older age bands.

How divisions and promotion work

Each age band is split into divisions, with stronger teams sitting higher. At season's end, the top teams in a division are promoted and the bottom sides are relegated, so a newly promoted team should expect tougher fixtures week to week. Fixtures, results and tables are published on Full-Time, the FA's results service, which is where parents look each Sunday. How does a new club register with the Dorset Youth Football League?

Step 1: County FA affiliation

Before the league will consider you, your club needs to affiliate with the Dorset County FA. Affiliation covers your club as a whole, and every team secretary and coach will need an FA number tied to the club. If you are a newly formed club, budget time for this: affiliation opens in early summer and you cannot complete league entry without it. Your welfare officer details will be checked at this stage too, so have that person lined up before you start.

Step 2: League entry application and deadlines

Once affiliated, you apply directly to the Dorset Youth Football League for entry, usually through the league's own online form during the summer window. Newly promoted teams and brand-new clubs should flag their status on the form, because it affects which division you are placed in. Miss the deadline and you are waiting a full season, so put the date in your phone the moment the league announces it. If you are unsure where your Under 7s or Under 10s sit, ask the league secretary directly rather than guessing; they would rather answer the question than untangle a misplaced team in September.

Step 3: Team and official details

Your entry application asks for team age groups, proposed divisions, and the details of each team secretary, coach and welfare officer. Get these right first time, because the league uses them for fixture lists and safeguarding checks. Once entry is confirmed, keep a copy of what you submitted; you will retype those names and dates repeatedly through the season otherwise. What are the player eligibility and registration rules for youth teams in Dorset?

Registration windows and deadlines

The Dorset Youth Football League sets an annual registration window before the season starts, and clubs affiliated through Dorset FA submit player details through the FA's system. Missing the window means a player sits out until a late registration is accepted, so get your squad list to your club secretary early. The FA publishes guidance on youth player registration that covers the county-level process.

Squad sizes and age verification

Squad sizes follow the FA's youth format rules: smaller sides at Under 7s to Under 11s, growing to 11v11 at older ages. Proof of age is a photo of a birth certificate or passport uploaded at registration, checked against the player's age group. Keep a copy on your phone for matchday spot checks.

Transfers and dual registration

Players can move between clubs mid-season, but a transfer needs consent from both clubs and the league before the player is eligible. Dual registration lets one player appear for two teams under set conditions, useful when your squad is thin.

How are fixtures and results published and managed in the league?

Fixture release and kick-off times

Fixtures are issued by the league in blocks, usually a few weeks ahead, and published on the league's Full-Time pages. Your team's fixtures list kick-off times and venues, but Sunday morning slots are set in consultation with your club because pitches are shared across age groups. Once a fixture is confirmed, treat the time as fixed unless the league or your club agrees a change.

Submitting results and match cards

The home team typically enters the result on Full-Time shortly after full time, and both teams complete the match card, confirming the score, scorers where required, and player registrations. Cards have a deadline, commonly within a few days of the game, and late cards can mean a fine, so it is worth doing it Sunday evening while the details are fresh. Check your league handbook for the exact cut-off.

Postponements and rearrangements

If weather or pitch unavailability forces a postponement, tell the league and the opposition as early as possible, then agree a new date with your fixtures secretary. Rearranged fixtures are added back to the schedule, and results only count once the rearranged game is played and carded.

What safeguarding and welfare requirements apply to Dorset youth league clubs?

Every affiliated club must meet FA safeguarding standards before the season starts: a named Club Welfare Officer, in-date DBS checks for all coaches and regular volunteers, and a safeguarding policy the club can show on demand. League officials can ask to see these at any point, so keep the paperwork together rather than in the group chat.

Welfare officers and DBS checks

Each club needs at least one welfare officer, and youth clubs need two: one for the club and one committee-level lead. Both must complete an FA DBS check and the welfare officer training course. If you are the parent-coach running an Under 7s side, your club secretary will usually arrange your DBS through the club; never start coaching without one being in progress.

Safeguarding children certification

Coaches and volunteers must complete the FA's Safeguarding Children course, renewed every three years. The FA's youth football regulations set the wider framework, including mini-soccer formats and squad rules, that county leagues like Dorset's operate within.

How does the Dorset league compare structurally with neighbouring county youth leagues like Cornwall or Mid Sussex?

What county leagues have in common

Most county youth leagues, Dorset included, run on the same skeleton: age groups from Under 7 upwards, small-sided formats for the youngest teams graduating to 11-a-side, divisions grouped by ability, and registration windows before the season starts. Safeguarding duties sit with clubs in every county, with a welfare officer expected at each one. Fixtures and results are published centrally by the league, usually through a results secretary or appointed registrar.

Where county leagues differ

The differences are mostly scale and geography. Cornwall covers a long, rural county with heavy travel between fixtures, while Mid Sussex and Medway operate in denser corridors where away trips stay short. Suffolk-based leagues vary by division too. For a volunteer coach, none of this changes your weekly routine: you still pick a line-up, message the group chat, and confirm the result. The league's structure shapes who you play, not how much admin lands on you.

Where can parents find fixtures, results and league tables for Dorset youth football?

The league's official channels

League fixtures, results and tables live on the FA's Full-Time system, which most county youth leagues — including neighbouring competitions like the Cornwall Youth Football League — use to publish division standings and match dates. Your club secretary or the league's own website will point you to the correct division page for your age group. Check it on Friday evening rather than Sunday morning: postponements and pitch changes get posted there first.

Getting matchday info without the group chat

The group chat still earns its keep for kick-off times and car pooling, but it should never be the source of truth for fixtures. If a WhatsApp message and the league's published fixture disagree, the published fixture wins.

What the league expects vs what the team needs

The Dorset Youth Football League handles registrations, fixtures and published results. It does not keep your line-ups, your player of the match picks, or the week the quiet winger scored her first goal. That record lives in the group chat, scattered across WhatsApp messages, and disappears by the time the end-of-season do comes round.

FAQ

What age can my child start playing in the Dorset Youth Football League?

Children can start in the Under 7 age band, which plays 5v5 mini-soccer on small pitches. The pathway then progresses through 7v7 and 9v9 before reaching full 11v11 football at Under 14 and above. Most clubs take registrations from around age five or six, so contact clubs the summer before your child turns seven.

How much does it cost to enter a team in the Dorset Youth Football League?

The league charges an entry fee per team, set annually and announced with the summer registration window, plus Dorset County FA affiliation costs. Your club will also face player registration fees and pitch or facility hire. Ask the league secretary for current figures when you submit your entry application, since amounts change season to season.

Can my child play for two different teams in the same season?

Yes, through dual registration, which lets one player appear for two teams under set conditions. It is useful when a squad is thin and needs extra cover. A mid-season move between two different clubs is different: that transfer needs consent from both clubs and the league before the player is eligible to play.

What happens if our match is called off on the morning of the game?

Tell the league and the opposition as early as possible, then agree a new date with your fixtures secretary. The rearranged fixture goes back on the schedule, and the result only counts once the game is actually played and the match card submitted. Postponements and pitch changes are posted on the league's Full-Time pages first.

Do away teams need to provide a referee in the Dorset Youth Football League?

The league's handbook sets referee arrangements, and practice varies by age group, with home clubs typically sourcing officials for their fixtures. If no referee is available, the two teams can agree a club volunteer to officiate on the day. Check your league handbook or ask the fixtures secretary before the season starts so you are not improvising on a Sunday morning.
