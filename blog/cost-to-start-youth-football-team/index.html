Setting up a grassroots youth football team in the UK costs roughly £1,000 to £2,500 in year one, plus around £500 to £1,500 a year to keep running. The mandatory FA affiliation, insurance and DBS checks are the cheap part; kit, pitch hire and coaching courses are where the budget actually goes.

What does it cost to start a youth football team in year one?

The short answer: a realistic year-one budget range

For a single grassroots youth team, expect roughly £1,500 to £3,500 in year one if you are starting under an existing club's umbrella, and considerably more if you are affiliating a brand-new club. The mandatory items are county FA affiliation and public liability insurance, which are priced per team and set by your county FA, plus player registration fees. The FA publishes affiliation guidance and fee structures through England Football's club affiliation pages, so check your county's rates before you budget. Everything else, from kit to a first-aid bag, scales with age group and ambition.

Who pays: the club, the coach or the parents?

Usually the parents, through monthly subs, with the club fronting affiliation and reclaiming it. If you are setting up independently, you pay upfront and recover costs later. Either way, track every pound from day one, and keep match results and payments visible to parents in one place rather than buried in the group chat. A simple team site like Touchline Tales handles that record-keeping, and our guide to sharing results with parents shows what that looks like in practice.

What are the mandatory costs before you can affiliate with the FA?

Before your team plays a single fixture, three bills land: affiliation to your County FA, insurance, and player registration with your league. They are unavoidable and they come at once, usually in June or July, so budget for them before you buy a single ball.

County FA affiliation and league registration fees

Affiliation fees vary by County FA and age group, typically running from around £50 to £150 for a youth team. League registration is usually a separate charge of roughly £20 to £50. Treat these as illustrative ranges and check your own County FA's schedule, since London and northern counties can differ sharply.

Public liability and personal accident insurance

Affiliation includes public liability cover, but most leagues require personal accident insurance for every registered player. This is usually bought through the County FA's preferred scheme and typically costs a few pounds per player, so a squad of 14 might pay £50 to £100 in total.

Player registration with the league

Most youth leagues charge per player to register, often £5 to £15 each. For a squad of 14, expect £70 to £210.

What qualifications and DBS checks do you need and what does it cost?

Introduction to Coaching Football and safeguarding

The FA's Introduction to Coaching Football is the standard entry course for a new volunteer coach. It costs £260 (or £130 in some counties with subsidy) and includes the Safeguarding Children Course, so you can tick both boxes in one enrolment. Check current pricing on England Football's Introduction to Coaching Football page before you book, as county FAs occasionally part-fund places.

DBS checks for volunteer coaches

An FA DBS check is mandatory for anyone coaching under-18s. For volunteers it is free through your county FA; you will need to verify your ID with your club welfare officer. Keep a copy of the certificate, as leagues will ask for it at registration.

First aid qualifications

The Introduction to Coaching Football includes an online first aid module, but many clubs also want an in-person IFAiF (Introduction to First Aid in Football) certificate. Some county FAs run these free or at low cost for volunteers, so ask before paying full price. Budget roughly £0–£300 across all three requirements, and get your club to reimburse where possible. Your welfare officer can confirm exactly which certificates your league requires; our welfare officer checklist covers the paperwork.

How much does kit, equipment and pitch hire cost in the first season?

Kit: shirts, shorts, socks and spare shirts

A full strip for a squad of 14 to 16 players, including a keeper shirt and a couple of spares, typically lands between £400 and £900 depending on whether you go matched or mix-and-match. Add numbered shirts if your league requires them, and budget £50 to £100 for a spare plain kit in a contrasting colour for clashes. Socks and shorts are cheap per player but add up fast once you order for the whole squad.

Training equipment and matchday kit bag

Balls, bibs, cones, a bag of pumps and a decent first aid kit will run £150 to £300 in year one. A matchday bag with water bottles, a whistle and a small first aid kit is worth putting together once and forgetting about.

Pitch hire, match fees and referee costs

If your club has its own ground this line shrinks, but many new teams rent school or council pitches. Rates vary wildly by council, so treat any figure here as illustrative and get a quote locally. The one paragraph worth pinning above your desk: pitch hire is the cost that behaves like a subscription, not a purchase. You pay it every single week, home or away, rain or shine, and it quietly becomes the biggest running item after kit.

A team playing 20 home fixtures at even £30 a session is looking at £600 a season before a single referee fee, and referees for youth football commonly add £20 to £40 per match. Ask the club secretary early whether match fees are charged per fixture or folded into an annual pitch arrangement, because the answer changes your budget by hundreds of pounds. Whatever you collect in subs, record it somewhere the welfare officer and parents can see the season at a glance rather than buried in the group chat; keeping that record in the club's own colours and crest, as we do at Touchline Tales, makes treasurer questions at the end of term a two-minute job.

What are the ongoing monthly and annual running costs once the team is established?

Annual renewals: affiliation, insurance, league fees

Year two repeats the big three: county FA affiliation, public liability and personal accident insurance, and your league's registration fee. Expect these to land together in the summer window before the season starts, so budget for one lump sum rather than monthly spread. Your FA-checked volunteer status also renews on a cycle, though the DBS check itself stays free for volunteers in football eligibility, per GOV.UK guidance on DBS checks.

Consumables and kit replacement

Balls, bibs, cones and first-aid supplies wear out every season, and growing Under 8s will outgrow their shirts before the stitching goes. Match balls and a replenished medical kit are the two lines most teams replace annually.

Communication and team admin tools

The group chat is free but chaotic. Dedicated tools vary: Spond, Pitchero and Touchline Tales all offer a free tier for core team messaging, with paid plans adding features like club branding. How do costs differ by age group, from mini-soccer to youth 11-a-side?

Under 6s to Under 8s: the cheapest entry point

At this age football is five-a-side on a small pitch with pop-up goals, so you need little more than a bag of balls, bibs and cones. Many teams train at a local park or school field for free. Referee fees are rarely a factor because matches are often refereed by a volunteer parent. Budget for kit and basics and you can run the season on a shoestring.

Under 9s to Under 11s: more players, more kit

Squads grow to seven or nine a side, so you need a bigger kit order, a match ball per age group and possibly a small pitch booking with goals supplied. Referees start being appointed, and match fees typically land on the home team. Expect your budget to step up noticeably here.

Youth 11-a-side: full pitch, full costs

From Under 12s up you are hiring a full-size pitch, paying per-match referee fees every week and replacing more kit as players grow. Add training-floodlight hire in winter and this is the most expensive stage of a team's life, which is why subs usually rise with each age group.

How can you fund or offset the cost through subs, sponsorship and grants?

Setting realistic monthly subs

Most teams cover affiliation, pitch hire and kit through monthly subs, typically £3–£5 per session for mini-soccer, though the figure varies by region and what the club provides. Charge for a term rather than a month and you build a small buffer for the unexpected. Registration fees levied by your County FA and league are usually paid up front, so collect them before the season starts rather than absorbing them yourself.

Local sponsorship and shirt deals

A local plumber, estate agent or café will often fund a full kit in exchange for their name on the shirt, usually £200–£500 for the season. Ask parents first: someone on the touchline often owns the business you need. A one-page pitch with your player numbers and matchday attendance makes the ask concrete.

Grants and funding schemes for grassroots clubs

The Football Foundation, County FA small grants and supermarket schemes like Tesco's community grants fund kit, goals and coaching courses. Applications are short but take weeks, so apply in the close season. Keep receipts from day one; most funders reimburse rather than pay directly.

What hidden or unexpected costs catch out new team founders?

Tournaments, trophies and end-of-season costs

Summer tournament entries typically run £25–£50 per team, and most sides enter two or three. Add a player of the match award most weeks, end-of-season trophies or medals at £5–£10 a head, and a presentation-day venue. Budget £150–£300 for the season's send-off, or it comes out of your own pocket.

Away travel and refreshments

You'll pay match fees for away fixtures, and someone buys the half-time drinks and post-match snacks for both teams most weeks. For parents, fuel to a ground 40 minutes away is a real cost too. A small refreshments float of £50–£100 covers the season if you rotate who brings it.

Admin time: the cost nobody budgets for

Your Sunday nights go on line-ups, fixture confirmations and chasing replies in the group chat. That's unpaid hours, every week, for nine months. FAQ

Can I start a youth football team with no money upfront?

Not really. Affiliation, insurance and player registration must be paid before your first fixture, typically £190 to £510 combined, and they land in June or July. You can minimise outlay by joining an existing club, applying for close-season grants, or collecting registration money from parents before you spend it, but some upfront cash is unavoidable.

Do I need to set up a limited company or bank account for a junior football team?

No. Most grassroots teams run as unincorporated community groups affiliated to a County FA, with no company registration needed. A dedicated club bank account is still worth opening, because county FAs and leagues usually require one for affiliation and grant applications, and it keeps subs and receipts separate from a volunteer's personal account.

How much should parents pay in monthly subs for a grassroots team?

Most teams charge £3 to £5 per session for mini-soccer, though the figure varies by region and what the club provides. Charge by the term rather than the month to build a small buffer, and collect affiliation and league registration fees up front so the founder is not absorbing them personally.

Is it cheaper to join an existing club than to start a new team?

Yes, usually. Running under an existing club's umbrella means shared pitch arrangements, existing affiliation, welfare officers and kit suppliers, which is why the year-one estimate drops to roughly £1,500 to £3,500 for a single team. Affiliating a brand-new club means paying the mandatory fees yourself and sourcing pitches, goals and equipment from scratch.

What insurance do I legally need to run a youth football team?

County FA affiliation includes public liability cover, which is mandatory. Most leagues also require personal accident insurance for every registered player, usually bought through the County FA's preferred scheme at a few pounds per head, so roughly £50 to £100 for a squad of 14. Confirm the exact requirements with your league before the season starts.
