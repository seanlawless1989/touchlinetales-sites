Key takeaways

  • A 10-15cm resin figure or a £1-2 medal suits weekly grassroots presentations better than a large one-off cup.
  • Budget per week across a roughly 30-week season, ordering for about 25 presentations plus spares rather than one trophy per fixture.
  • Standard engraving turnaround is 3 to 5 working days, so order base trophies in August and keep spare or blank plates on the shelf.

Pick a trophy around 15-20cm tall with a reusable engraving plate, or a bulk pack of medals at roughly £1-2 each, and budget the whole season's player of the match trophies in one order of 25-30. Then run the same 60-second presentation every week. This guide gives you the sizes, turnaround times and per-week maths to get it right once.

What size player of the match trophy suits a weekly grassroots award?

For a weekly award, most clubs pick something in the 10–15 cm range: small enough for a six-year-old to hold one-handed, cheap enough to replace every Sunday, and shelf-friendly at home. UK suppliers like Trophy.com list youth football awards in exactly this bracket, with plastic and resin figures far cheaper than cup-style metalware.

Why small and repeatable beats big and one-off

A 30 cm cup presented once a season impresses nobody's seven-year-old as much as something they keep every week. Weekly awards work on cost and routine, not size: a modest trophy every match beats one giant one in December. Log each winner's name and date on a team site like Touchline Tales so the season's story survives after the shelf gets crowded.

Should your club hand out trophies or player of the match medals?

When medals win. Medals cost less per presentation, so a 30-week season stays affordable even if you buy enough for every squad member. They also go home with the child the same day, which matters more to an Under 8 than anything else. A medal on a ribbon gets worn to school on Monday, and that is the real prize. When a trophy wins. A trophy has weight and height, and a seven-year-old holding something above their head feels different from wearing something round their neck. Trophies also carry names and dates better, so they build a visible season record. The trade-offs are real: they cost more per week, they need shelf space at home, and if you hand the same trophy back each week you need a system for who has it and when it returns.

A trophy on repeat works like this: buy one decent cup, engrave a plate each week or keep a running list, and photograph the winner with it before it goes to the next home. The photo is what lasts. Post it to your team site so parents who missed the match see it, and keep a season-long record child by child rather than losing it in the group chat (https://touchlinetales.club/guides/share-results-with-parents/). The honest downside: a returned trophy means chasing one family every Sunday night, and a lost cup mid-season is your problem to fix.

A hybrid: medals weekly, trophy at season end. Hand out a cheap medal each week, then award one engraved trophy per player at the end-of-season do. You get the weekly routine, the Monday-morning badge, and a keepsake worth keeping, without paying trophy prices thirty times over.

How does engraving work and how long does it take?

Engraving options compared

Most trophy shops offer three routes: laser engraving on the plate or cup, rotary engraving on metal plates, and self-adhesive plates you can overstick each week. Laser suits glass and coated metal; rotary suits solid brass and aluminium plates. For a weekly player of the match award, a blank plate plus a reusable clip-on or magnetic engraved name plate is the cheapest repeatable setup, because the trophy itself never needs re-engraving.

Turnaround: what to plan for

Standard engraving turnaround at UK trophy retailers is typically 3 to 5 working days, with express services charging a premium for 24 to 48 hours, according to one engraver's published guidance on engraving lead times. Order your season's base trophies in August, before the first fixture pile-up, and keep spare plates on the shelf.

The blank-plate workaround

Buy the cup or shield with an empty plate, then engrave a full-season name plate per week in one batch. If Sunday night arrives and nothing is engraved, hand the winner a medal and a written certificate, update the season record on your team site, and backfill the plate at month end. Keep the club welfare officer's requirements in mind too when photos of presentations go on the site (welfare officer checklist).

What does a player of the match trophy cost per week across a season?

Worked example: a 30-week season

Budget per week, not per trophy. A typical grassroots season runs around 30 weeks, so divide the total spend by 30 to see what presentation week actually costs.

Illustrative maths: a £12 trophy bought once and reused all season costs 40p per week. A £3 medal handed out weekly costs £90 across the season, or £3 per week. A hybrid, say a monthly trophy plus weekly medals, lands somewhere in between. These prices are illustrative; check current supplier pricing before you commit.

The trap is buying weekly trophies for every match. That is £360-plus at the same illustrative price, and you end up with 30 near-identical trophies nobody displays.

Who pays: club funds, subs or parents

Three routes, in order of least friction. Club funds cover it if you have a float; ask your treasurer at the start of the season. Weekly subs can absorb a few pounds per month without anyone noticing. Asking parents to chip in works, but collect once in September, not every Sunday night. Whichever you pick, agree it before week one so the presentation never stalls on money.

How do you order player of the match awards in bulk without overbuying?

Sizing a bulk order

Count your weeks first, not your players. A typical grassroots season runs roughly 30 weeks, but cups, weather call-offs and pitch closures mean you will present fewer times than the fixture list promises. Order for around 25 presentations and keep one spare trophy in the kit bag for the week you lose one.

When single ordering wins

If your team plays irregularly, if you rotate between medals and trophies, or if you present a shared award some weeks, bulk ordering leaves you with stock nobody wants. Single reorders cost more per unit but match what you actually hand out. Under 7s to Under 11s squads especially, where FA rules favour fair playing time over results, often benefit from buying small and adjusting.

Reordering fast mid-season

Pick a supplier with a published engraving turnaround before the season starts, so a top-up order lands before Sunday. Even the Premier League runs its player of the match award on a weekly rhythm (Premier League), and your routine should survive a lost trophy the same way theirs does: reorder, don't redesign.

What should a manager say when presenting the player of the match award?

Keep it short, warm and specific. A script that works for Under 8s and Under 14s alike has three parts: name the moment, name the player, hand it over.

Try this, under a minute:

"Right, player of the match this week. There were a few contenders — [name] never stopped running, and [name] kept talking at the back. But this week it goes to [player]. First goal of the season, and he stayed on his feet when everyone around him was losing theirs. Come on up, [player]."

Swap the reason each week so it means something: a first goal, a clean sheet, fair playing time earned by covering for a teammate, or simply effort at training paying off. Two reasons beat five.

One tip: write the name and reason on your phone at full time, before the WhatsApp messages bury them. You will thank yourself at presentation.

How do you set up a repeatable weekly player of the match presentation routine?

The five-minute matchday routine

Decide the winner before you leave the pitch, not on the drive home. Pick three moments from the game, name the winner out loud to the squad, and let them applaud. The FA's safeguarding guidance encourages presentations that build confidence without singling anyone out for criticism, so frame the award around effort and one specific moment (FA safeguarding guidance). Write the name and the moment on your phone there and then, because Sunday-night memory is unreliable.

Rotate who hands the trophy over: a parent one week, the previous winner the next. It keeps the routine fresh and spreads the small jobs that volunteer managers already carry.

Keeping a season record parents can see

A note in the group chat disappears by Tuesday. Keep a running list instead: week number, opponent, winner, the reason. Our own site, Touchline Tales, does this on a team page parents can check, with each award kept child by child across the season. Its honest gap: it records the season, it does not order trophies for you, so the routine above still needs your kit of awards ready each week.

What do Premier League player of the match trophies look like, and should you copy them?

The Premier League player of the match award is a small rectangular glass or crystal plaque on a stand, roughly 20cm tall, with the match details etched on the front. Broadcasters hand it over pitchside seconds after full time, which is why it is a plaque rather than a cup.

Copying it is impractical for a weekly grassroots award. That style of engraved glass costs several times what a resin cup or medal does, and most engravers take days rather than minutes.

Borrow the idea instead: a consistent award, presented straight after full time, with the reason said out loud. A £5 cup done the same way every week carries the same weight for an Under 9.

FAQ

Can I get a player of the match trophy engraved same week for Sunday presentation?

Standard engraving turnaround runs 3 to 5 working days, with express services at 24 to 48 hours for a premium, so same-week is possible if you order by midweek and pay the express fee. For a true Sunday deadline, keep a blank-plate trophy and hand out a medal plus certificate, then backfill the plate later.

How many player of the match medals should I order for an Under 8s squad?

Count weeks, not players. A season runs roughly 30 weeks but weather and call-offs cut that down, so order for about 25 presentations plus a couple of spares. At £1-2 per medal, a bulk pack of 25-30 keeps the whole season covered in one order for £50 or less.

Do girls' and boys' teams need different player of the match awards?

No. The same 10-15cm resin figure or ribbon medal works for any junior squad; suppliers sell youth awards unisex. What matters is consistency across your club, so mixed siblings and teams compare the same shelf piece. If anything, pick neutral colours and avoid pink-and-blue splits that age groups notice.

What is a fair way to pick the player of the match when everyone played well?

Name the winner before leaving the pitch, pick one specific moment, and frame it around effort rather than results, which fits fair playing time expectations at junior ages. Rotate the presenter weekly, a parent one week, the previous winner the next, and track winners across the season so nobody goes unnoticed by December.

Are cheap plastic player of the match trophies worth buying?

Yes, for weekly presentation. A resin or plastic figure at 10-15cm costs a fraction of metalware, survives being carried in a school bag, and a modest trophy every week beats one giant cup in December. Save the metal cup budget for an end-of-season award, and check current supplier pricing before committing.
