A good player of the match certificate has five things: the player's name, the date and opponent, the club crest, a short reason for the award, and space for a signature. Get those right and a free printable beats anything you'd buy. This guide shows you what to include, wording borrowed from how UEFA and the ICC do it, and how to present it so an Under 8 treasures it as much as an open-age player.

What should a player of the match certificate include?

A certificate needs seven things: the player's full name, your club crest, the date, the opposition, the award title ("Player of the Match"), a signature line for the coach or manager, and space for a short reason. That last field matters most to a child. UEFA presents its own award with the competition branding and the player's name front and centre, which is why the UEFA competitions page is a useful reference for how a formal presentation looks.

The seven fields every certificate needs

Skip any of the seven and the certificate loses its meaning: without the opposition and date it could be from any match, without a signature it looks printed off, not awarded.

Design choices by age group

Under 7s to Under 11s want big crests, bright colours and one line of text. Open age players prefer something plain enough to frame. Keep the wording short either way.

Print size, paper and cheap options

A5 on card from any home printer works. Record each award in your team site alongside the fixture, so the season builds a history: Touchline Tales keeps every match and milestone, child by child, in your own crest and colours.

How do UEFA and ICC present their player of the match awards?

UEFA player of the match: the presentation ritual

UEFA hands the award over on the pitch, straight after full time, in front of both sets of supporters. A presenter names the player, reads a short reason for the choice, and hands over a small trophy while cameras roll. The whole thing takes under a minute.

ICC and IPL player of the match: what gets handed over

Cricket's format adds a physical takeaway: a medal-style award plus a cheque, presented at the post-match ceremony. The player speaks briefly into a microphone, usually one or two sentences. The physical object is what survives; the speech is forgotten by morning.

What translates to a Sunday league touchline

Three elements copy across for nothing: name the reason aloud, hand over something physical, keep it under a minute. An Under 8 cares about the handshake and the certificate in their hand, not the speech. If your club has a welfare officer, loop them in on how awards are presented to younger age groups; the club's welfare officer checklist covers safeguarding basics around photos and public praise.

How do you decide who gets player of the match in rugby and football?

Football: coach's decision vs the touchline vote

Most grassroots football coaches pick the player themselves, judging on effort and attitude as much as goals. Some clubs let parents or players vote in a WhatsApp poll after full time, which spreads the fun but can reward the loudest family rather than the best performance. A hybrid works well: the coach nominates three, then the squad votes.

Rugby: the player of the match tradition and who presents it

In rugby, naming a player of the match is formal tradition, and the opposition or a neutral often chooses, keeping it friendly between clubs. At professional level the award is presented on the pitch immediately after full time, as the ICC describes for its player of the match awards, with the recipient named and a trophy handed over in front of everyone. Copy that: name it loudly, hand it over straight away.

Rotating the award so it isn't the same three kids

Track who has won across the season and set a soft rule: nobody wins twice until everyone has won once. For Under 7s to Under 11s, weight the pick towards effort, first goals and improved positions, so fair playing time and progress count as much as the scoreline.

How does the BBC player of the match vote work and can clubs copy it?

How broadcast fan votes collect nominations

Broadcast votes keep it deliberately simple: a shortlist of three or four nominees is published, viewers pick one, and the winner is announced on air. There is no open nomination stage, no write-ins, and no debate. The narrow choice is what makes people respond, because voting takes seconds and there is a visible result. A grassroots version needs the same discipline: name three players who did well, not twenty.

Running a parent vote without the group chat chaos

The group chat version fails for familiar reasons: forty WhatsApp messages, three different answers, and nobody sure which vote counts. Instead, send one message with three named options and a deadline, collect replies by a single method, and announce the winner at full time or in the same thread. If your team site supports a simple vote or poll, run it there so the result sits with the match record rather than scrolling away. Tools like Spond also handle polls, though any one-message, one-deadline approach works.

What wording works on a player of the match certificate?

Formal template

Borrow the phrasing used at professional presentations: name the award, name the competition, name the recipient, state the reason. A grassroots version reads: "Presented to [Name], Player of the Match, [Team] vs [Opponent], [Date], for [reason]." The reason line is the one worth rewriting each week. "For two goals and tireless work up front" means more to a child than a generic "outstanding performance", and it gives parents something concrete to keep in the memory box.

Fun template for younger age groups

For Under 7s to Under 11s, swap formality for the moment. Try: "[Name] is our Player of the Match! [Reason] — awarded by [Team] on [Date]." Reasons that land with this age group include "first goal of the season", "bravest save", or "never stopped running". Keep the certificate to one memorable sentence; younger players read the reason aloud to their parents, not the decoration.

Rugby-specific wording variant

Rugby squads rotate honours more widely, so wording should reflect the whole-team ethic: "For relentless tackling and supporting every carry" works better than goal-based phrasing. If your team votes like the BBC's fan awards, where supporters pick the winner rather than a pundit, add "as voted by the squad" to the certificate. That line matters: it tells the player their teammates chose them, which carries weight a coach's pick cannot.

Where can clubs find a player of the match template?

Canva, Word and Google Docs all have free certificate templates you can adapt in an evening. The catch is that a generic template ignores your club's identity, so most coaches end up rebuilding one anyway.

What to check before you print

Confirm you have the right to use the club crest: if it was designed by a volunteer, ask the secretary rather than assuming. Use a font the club already uses for fixture cards, and keep editable fields for name, date, opponent and the reason the player won it. Print on card, not paper, because an Under 8 certificate gets folded into a school bag.

How should grassroots clubs present the award after a match?

Timing the presentation at full time

Present the award at full time, before the group chat scatters and parents start packing the car. A five-minute pause while everyone is still gathered gives the moment weight and lets the whole team clap the winner. Ask the captain or a teammate to hand it over rather than doing it yourself every week; the player hears it matters to the squad, not just the coach. Keep the words short and specific: name the moment, the first goal, the tackle, the fair playing time you noticed.

The photo and the season record

Take one photo of the winner holding the certificate, then check the club's photo routine against The FA's safeguarding guidance before anything goes online. Consent rules vary by club and age group, so let your welfare officer set the policy once rather than deciding each Sunday.

Then keep the record. A note of every winner, date and opposition, kept in the team site or even a simple spreadsheet, turns twenty certificates into a season story parents can look back on at the end-of-season do.

Should the award be recorded anywhere beyond the certificate?

From certificate to season record

A certificate is a moment; a season record is the story. When end-of-season awards arrive, a list of every player of the match winner, first scorer and most-improved note turns a guess into a fair decision, and it backs up the "fair playing time" conversations parents ask about. A spreadsheet works if you update it every Sunday, but the group chat never will: winners scroll away within days.

Honest limits of each approach

A spreadsheet is free and flexible, but it lives on one person's laptop and dies when that parent-coach steps down. Spond, Pitchero and similar tools track attendance and messages well, but a season-long awards record is not always front and centre. FAQ

Who officially presents the player of the match award in England at grassroots level?

There is no official body that presents it. In most grassroots football clubs the coach or manager chooses and presents the award, though some hand the job to the club captain or a teammate for extra weight. Rugby clubs often invite the opposition or a neutral to pick, which keeps the decision friendly between clubs.

Can I put my club's crest on a certificate template I found online?

Yes, but confirm you have the rights first. If the crest was designed by a volunteer, ask your club secretary rather than assuming it is free to use. Once cleared, keep editable fields for the name, date, opponent and reason, and print on card rather than paper, since a young player's certificate usually ends up folded in a school bag.

What do you write on a player of the match certificate for Under 7s?

Keep it to one memorable sentence: "[Name] is our Player of the Match! [Reason] — awarded by [Team] on [Date]." Reasons that land with this age include "first goal of the season", "bravest save" or "never stopped running". Younger players read the reason aloud to their parents, so the wording matters more than the decoration.

Is there a difference between player of the match and man of the match?

They describe the same award; "player of the match" is simply the modern, inclusive term now used by broadcasters and governing bodies. Grassroots clubs have largely followed suit, and the switch costs nothing. Whichever term you use, keep it consistent across certificates, your season record and any announcements, so parents and players know exactly what the honour is called.

How do other clubs handle parents disagreeing with the player of the match choice?

Common approaches include letting the coach nominate three players and having the squad vote, or running a single parent poll with named options and a deadline. A soft rotation rule also helps: nobody wins twice until everyone has won once. Specific reasons given aloud, tied to effort or a first goal, defuse most disputes before they start.
