Key takeaways

  • Most English grassroots league tables are published through the FA's Full-Time system at fulltime.thefa.com, updated only after a league results secretary confirms each result.
  • Results are entered by volunteer club secretaries and confirmed by league fixtures secretaries, so midweek lag is usually a late human submission, not a technical fault.

FA grassroots football league tables are published online through the FA's Full-Time system, which turns results entered by league secretaries into standings, fixtures and top scorers. This guide shows you where to find your table, how results get entered, why standings lag or go wrong, and the exact steps to get an incorrect entry fixed.

Where can I find FA grassroots football league tables online?

The FA Full-Time public site

Most grassroots league tables live on FA Full-Time, the FA's own system for publishing fixtures, results and standings. Search for your league by name, then bookmark your division's page. Results appear here once the league's results secretary has entered them, usually within a day or two of the weekend.

League and county FA websites

Many leagues run their own websites that pull standings straight from Full-Time, so the numbers match. Your county FA's site also lists affiliated leagues with links to each one. If a league has its own site, it will usually link to its Full-Time pages rather than maintain a separate table.

Finding your division when the league name is unfamiliar

Your Under 9s might play in a league with a sponsor's name attached, or a name nothing like your club's. Search Full-Time for your county plus "youth league", then scan the divisions list for your age group. Once found, save the link, because a team site that keeps every match and milestone works best alongside the official table rather than instead of it.

How does the FA Full-Time system update league tables?

Who enters results into Full-Time

Results are not typed in by the FA or by a central office. Each club's secretary or an appointed results officer submits the score, usually by a deadline the league sets, often within an hour or two of full time. The league's fixtures secretary then checks and confirms the result before it counts. That confirmation step is why responsibility sits with volunteers at club level, not with the FA itself.

When the table recalculates

The table only changes once a confirmed result is saved against the fixture. Full-Time recalculates standings from the stored results each time it publishes, so the table you see is a snapshot of confirmed entries, not a live feed. If your result is confirmed on Saturday but the league publishes updates on a schedule, Sunday's table may still show last week's points.

Why Sunday night results appear midweek

The lag is usually human, not technical. A results officer might submit on Saturday evening, but the fixtures secretary confirms in batches during the week, often alongside chasing missing results from away teams. Youth leagues also hold results for age groups where scores are not published at all, under FA rules for some Under 7s to Under 11s competitions. If you want parents to see the score before the table catches up, sharing it yourself works better: our guide on sharing results with parents (https://touchlinetales.club/guides/share-results-with-parents/) covers the simplest ways to do that.

What do the columns and abbreviations on a grassroots league table mean?

Most grassroots tables on FA Full-Time use the same short columns. Here is what each one means:

AbbreviationMeaning
PPlayed (all matches counted)
WWon
DDrawn
LLost
GFGoals for (scored)
GAGoals against (conceded)
GDGoal difference (GF minus GA)
PtsPoints: 3 for a win, 1 for a draw

Fair play and deduction columns

Some leagues add columns for disciplinary points or cards, reflecting the FA's respect agenda. Leagues can also apply point deductions for unplayed fixtures or unregistered players, and clubs are responsible for reporting results promptly under county FA guidance on results reporting.

When two teams are level on points

Tables usually split ties by goal difference, then goals scored, then head-to-head results. Check your league's published rules, because the order varies and some youth leagues use average points per game for unplayed fixtures.

Why is a grassroots league table wrong or out of date?

Because Full-Time only knows what league volunteers type into it, and those volunteers are parents with day jobs.

Results entered late or not at all

Most leagues require the home team to submit the score within a set window, often 48 hours, and the away team to confirm it. If a manager forgets on a Sunday night, the table sits frozen until someone chases it. A missing result is the single most common reason a table looks wrong midweek.

Postponements and unplayed fixtures

A postponed match stays on the system as a scheduled fixture until the league reschedules it. If nobody marks it as postponed, or the rearranged date never gets entered, the table shows games in hand that will never resolve. Some fixtures quietly vanish from the season entirely.

Wrong score or wrong team entered

Typing 3–1 instead of 1–3, or selecting the wrong division or age group, happens every week. The correction is easy but only if someone spots it, which is why you should check your team's row after every matchday.

Youth leagues and non-published tables

Many youth leagues deliberately withhold standings for Under 7s to Under 11s, in line with FA guidance on not publishing results at younger ages. If your table is blank, that is usually policy, not an error. Welfare officers can read more in our welfare officer checklist.

How do results and fixtures get entered into Full-Time?

The club's responsibility on matchday

Someone from your club, usually the manager or a nominated helper, records the score, scorers and any cards before leaving the ground. Most leagues ask clubs to submit the result through Full-Time itself, either from the touchline via mobile or later that evening. If your league uses the FA's Matchday app, the opposing manager confirms the same details, and the two entries are matched.

The league secretary's role

The results secretary checks submissions, chases discrepancies between the two clubs, and publishes the result so the table recalculates. They also handle postponements, and they are the person who can override a wrong entry. The Football Association of Wales publishes guidance for its grassroots leagues on how results feed into standings (https://www.faw.cymru/), and English leagues run the same way.

Typical deadlines and what happens if you miss them

Leagues commonly set a deadline of 24 to 48 hours after full time; illustrative examples range from same-evening submission to midweek cutoffs. Miss it and you risk a fine or, worse, a result that never appears, which is why many coaches keep their own season record as backup. How can a club or league secretary fix an incorrect table entry?

Step 1: check your own match record first

Before you email anyone, confirm the error from your side. Pull up your own record of the game: the match card or team sheet, the final score you texted the group chat, and any referee's report. If your Under 9s won 3-2 but the table shows 2-2, note exactly what the table says versus what happened on the pitch.

Step 2: contact the results secretary

Every league has a results secretary or fixtures secretary who owns the data in Full-Time. Send them one message with the fixture date, division, both team names, the score shown on the table, and the score you believe is correct. Attach evidence: a photo of the match card signed by both managers or the referee's card. Do not post it in the WhatsApp group and assume it will travel; the secretary needs it directly, in writing, ideally within a few days of the fixture.

Step 3: what happens after a correction

The secretary amends the result in Full-Time and the table recalculates automatically, usually within a day or two of the next update cycle. If nothing changes after a week, chase once, politely, with your original message attached. If the error sits at the club's end, a missed result submission for example, expect the league's rules on late submissions to apply, which can mean a fine or the result standing as recorded. Keep your own season record throughout so a disputed entry is a two-minute check, not a Sunday-night scramble.

Do grassroots leagues in Wales and Hull use the same table system?

Mostly yes, but not automatically. Welsh clubs do not sit under the FA Full-Time setup used in England, because Welsh grassroots football runs under the Football Association of Wales and its own league structures, so a table you find for a Welsh league is published by that league's own system rather than Full-Time.

Grassroots football in Wales

If your team plays in Wales, check your league's own website or the FAW competition pages for standings. The column meanings are the same, but the publishing system and update timing are decided locally, so the "who enters results" answer differs from an English county league.

Grassroots football in Hull and the East Riding

Hull and East Riding leagues do use the FA's Full-Time system, like most English grassroots leagues. The Hull Boys Sunday Football League publishes its fixtures, results and tables through Full-Time, so the same lag and correction processes covered earlier in this article apply. To check any league, open its table page: a Full-Time league shows the familiar FAWEB-style layout and a "Full-Time" logo, while leagues on other systems will look different.

How can a coach keep a season record when the league table only shows points?

What the table does not capture

Full-Time shows points, position and results, nothing else. It has no room for who played, who scored their first goal, or whether playing time has been fair across the squad. By May, most volunteer coaches struggle to remember September.

FAQ

How often are grassroots football league tables updated?

Tables on FA Full-Time recalculate as soon as a results secretary publishes a result, which typically happens within a day or two of the weekend. If a result is missing, the table stays frozen until it is entered or chased, so a midweek table that has not moved is usually waiting on a late submission rather than a system fault.

Can parents see youth league tables online, or are some hidden?

Some are hidden on purpose. Many youth leagues withhold standings for Under 7s to Under 11s, following FA guidance on not publishing results at younger ages, so a blank table is policy rather than an error. From around Under 12 upwards, tables are generally published through Full-Time or the league's own site, and your league's rules will confirm its approach.

What does Pld mean on a football league table?

Pld means played: the total number of matches a team has completed, wins, draws and losses combined. Some tables abbreviate it as just P. It sits alongside W, D and L, which break the total down, and it matters because teams with fewer played games have games in hand rather than a worse record.

Who do I contact if my team's result is missing from the table?

Contact your league's results secretary directly, in writing. Include the fixture date, division, both team names, the score shown on the table or the fact it is missing, the correct score, and evidence such as a photo of the match card signed by both managers. Do not rely on a WhatsApp group message to reach them.

Is there a grassroots football app that shows league tables and fixtures together?

Yes. The FA's Matchday app handles fixtures, result submission and confirmation for leagues using Full-Time, and the Full-Time public site shows tables and fixtures together.
