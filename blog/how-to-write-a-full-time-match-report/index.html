A full-time match report is a short written summary of your Sunday fixture that you submit through the FA's Full-Time system for league officials and publish for parents. This guide shows you exactly what to write, in what order, with a copy-and-paste template and side-by-side weak and strong examples of the same game, so the whole job takes fifteen minutes instead of a Sunday night.

What is a full-time match report and who actually reads it?

A full-time match report is the short summary you enter on the FA's Full-Time portal, or email to your league secretary, after the final whistle. It confirms the score, scorers and any discipline so the league can publish the result. Most leagues expect it within 24 to 48 hours.

Three audiences, three jobs for one document

League officials check it for an accurate result. Parents who missed the game want two sentences on how their child's team got on. And your future self wants a record of the season, because WhatsApp messages vanish and nobody remembers who scored the first goal by March.

The trick is writing once for all three: a factual core for the league, a sentence or two of colour for parents. Publishing to parents is covered in our guide to sharing results.

What structure should a grassroots match report follow?

The six-part skeleton

Every full-time match report a league official or parent expects follows the same six parts, in this order:

  1. Scoreline — teams, age group, final score.
  2. Scorers with minutes — who scored and roughly when.
  3. Key moments — two or three turning points, not a blow-by-blow account.
  4. Player of the match, with a reason — "for two brilliant saves in the second half", not just a name.
  5. Fair-play note — one line on how the game was played, which matters to the welfare officer.
  6. Thanks — to the opposition, the referee, and any parent who ran the line or brought the oranges.

How long each part should be

Keep the whole thing under 200 words. The scoreline and scorers take one line each. Key moments get two or three sentences. Player of the match, fair-play note, and thanks get a sentence apiece. Parents read match reports on their phones in the group chat or on a team site like Touchline Tales, so short paragraphs beat long ones. If you can write it in ten minutes on a Sunday night, the structure is working.

How do you write a match report when you only saw part of the game?

Most volunteer coaches miss chunks of their own matches: late arrival, a rotation that means nobody tracked the goals, or a Sunday split between two pitches. Write honestly from what you have. The scoreline, your half-time note, and parents' photo timestamps will reconstruct the rest.

The half-time note trick

At half time, type three lines into your phone: score, scorers so far, and one moment worth keeping ("Tom's first goal", "great save from Ava"). That takes thirty seconds and gives you the spine of the report. Add the second half the same way at full time, and you have the whole shape before you reach the car.

What to do when you genuinely don't know who scored

Say so: "Unsure who got our second, parents, help me out." Parents will happily fill gaps. If you use photos, remember FA guidance on safeguarding in youth football covers taking and sharing images of children, so check club policy and parental consent before posting FA safeguarding guidance.

What tone works for youth and grassroots match reports?

Write like a parent talking to other parents, not a journalist. Celebrate effort, first goals, brave saves and players who tried a new position. For Under 7s to Under 11s, avoid publishing scorelines that humiliate; a heavy defeat in print sticks with children far longer than with adults. FA guidance for young age groups leans this way, and welfare officers notice.

Tone rules by age group

  • Under 6 to Under 11: focus on moments, not the score. "Everyone got fair playing time and Maya made her first goal" beats "lost 9-0".
  • Under 12 to Under 16: the result matters, but keep it proportionate. Praise work rate alongside the win.
  • Open age: fuller match reporting is fine, but stay generous about the opposition.

Naming children safely

Name every child who featured where possible, first name plus surname initial is the usual grassroots convention, and check your club's photo and naming policy with your welfare officer. A child who never appears in any report notices as much as one who always does.

Worked example: a weak report and a strong report of the same game

The weak version

U9 Blues 3 – 2 U9 Reds. Great game today, well done lads. Player of the match: Tommy. Thanks to all the parents for getting the boys there in the rain. Training as normal Thursday.

The strong version

Under 9 Blues 3, Under 9 Reds 2 (HT 1–1)

The Blues came from behind twice on a heavy pitch. After a scrappy first half, Reds led through a well-worked corner. Leo equalised direct from a free kick, his first goal for the club, and the whole team mobbed him. Reds retook the lead early in the second half before Sam's low finish made it 2–2. With five minutes left, keeper Maya tipped a shot onto the bar and Tommy ran the length of the pitch to score the winner. Player of the match: Maya, for two saves that kept us in it. Every player got at least half a game. Thanks to Reds for a fair, friendly fixture.

What changed and why

The strong version follows a pattern you can copy in five minutes on a Sunday night: scoreline and half-time score up top, then a timeline of moments with names attached, then player of the match with a reason, then playing time and a thank-you. Names and moments are what parents read for; "great game, well done" gives them nothing to share. One caution applies to both versions: the FA's safeguarding guidance asks clubs to think carefully about naming children alongside images online, so keep published reports to first names only, or check your club's policy with your welfare officer first.

How do you publish a match report on Full-Time?

Deadlines and who enters what

Full-Time is where the result goes, not the prose. Your league's fixture secretary will tell you who enters results: some leagues make the home team manager do it, others split it so both sides confirm. Log into the league's Full-Time portal with the login they issue you, pick the fixture, and enter the score plus scorers if the league asks for them. Most leagues set a deadline of a day or two after the match, and some fine teams for late entries, so ask your secretary what yours is and set a Sunday-night reminder.

Where the prose version lives

Full-Time typically holds results and basic stats, not your write-up. The longer report belongs on your club site or the league website if it accepts reports. Write it once, paste the result into Full-Time, and post the full version where parents actually read.

How do you share a match report with parents and the wider club?

You have three routes: paste it into the WhatsApp group, publish it on a team page, or file it where the league can see it. Leagues using Full-Time publish results and reports on their own public pages, so your report can live beyond Sunday night (see FA Full-Time).

Building a season record child by child

A team page keeps every report in one place, so by April you can point a parent to their child's first goal or player of the match mentions without digging through chat. That record is also what a new welfare officer or next season's coach inherits. Touchline Tales does this by default: each report sits on the team site under the club's crest and colours, and the season builds itself. The honest gap: it does not file reports into Full-Time for you, so you still paste there yourself.

Spond, Pitchero and My-Team Sports all offer team pages too, so compare on how easily a report becomes a season archive rather than another post in a feed.

What common mistakes make a match report unusable?

Mistakes league officials catch

No scoreline is the most common failure. Full-Time needs a result, so a report that says "a hard-fought game" with no final score gives the league secretary nothing to check against. Wrong dates, wrong opposition names, and missing age group details also trigger corrections. If your report says you played "the Rovers" but Full-Time lists "Rovers FC U10," someone has to fix it by hand.

Mistakes parents notice

A report that names only the scorer ignores everyone else. Parents of the other fourteen players read it once and never again. Missing player of the match is a close second: the kids ask about it, so leaving it out is noticed immediately. Tone problems matter too; sarcasm about the referee or listing every conceded goal travels badly in a group chat.

Finally, reports buried in WhatsApp are effectively lost. Nobody scrolls back three weeks to find Sunday's write-up, which is why the season ends with no record at all.

FAQ

How soon after the game should I submit my full-time match report?

Most leagues expect the result within 24 to 48 hours of the final whistle, and some fine teams for late entries. Ask your fixture secretary what your league's deadline is, then set a Sunday-night reminder. The write-up itself takes fifteen minutes if you jot the score and scorers at half time.

Do I need parental permission to name children in a match report?

Check your club's policy with your welfare officer first. The usual grassroots convention is first name plus surname initial, and FA safeguarding guidance asks clubs to think carefully about naming children alongside images online. Keep published reports to first names only unless your club says otherwise, and make sure every child who featured gets a mention.

What should I write for player of the match when everyone played well?

Rotate it and always attach a reason. Name a child who tried something new, made a brave save, or kept their shape, then say why in one sentence. A bare name gives parents nothing, and a child who never appears in any report notices as much as one who always does.

Can I write a match report for Under 6s and Under 7s where there are no official results?

Yes, but focus on moments rather than the score. "Everyone got fair playing time and Maya made her first goal" beats a heavy defeat in print, which sticks with children far longer than with adults. For the youngest age groups, celebrate first goals, brave saves and effort, and keep any scoreline proportionate or leave it out.

Who is responsible for entering the result on Full-Time, the manager or the club secretary?

It varies by league: some make the home team manager do it, others split it so both sides confirm the fixture. Your fixture secretary will tell you which applies. Whoever does it logs into the league's Full-Time portal with the issued login, picks the fixture, and enters the score plus scorers if asked.
