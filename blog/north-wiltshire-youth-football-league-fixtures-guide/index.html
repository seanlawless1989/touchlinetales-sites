What is the North Wiltshire Youth Football League and which age groups does it cover?

The North Wiltshire Youth Football League is a grassroots football league for youth teams based around North Wiltshire, affiliated to the county FA and run by volunteers. It exists so that clubs in the area can play organised, sanctioned fixtures week to week rather than arranging friendlies ad hoc.

It serves youth age groups from Under 7s upwards, following the FA's youth football structure, with smaller-sided formats at the youngest ages building towards 11v11 as children get older. If you are a volunteer coach taking an Under 8s side into league football for the first time, this is the kind of league your club secretary will register the team with.

Who runs the league and how do you contact the committee?

The league is run by a volunteer committee: a chairman, secretary, fixtures secretary, registration secretary and welfare officer, each handling a slice of the season's admin. For fixture queries, player registrations or safeguarding questions, the committee's contact details are published on the league's pages, and most leagues like this now publish fixtures and results through the FA's Full-Time system, which is also where you can verify a team's division and results.

How do you find North Wiltshire Youth Football League fixtures and results?

Fixtures for the North Wiltshire Youth Football League live in three places: the league's own site, the FA's Full-Time system, and your club's channels. Full-Time is the official record — each division has a page listing every fixture, kickoff time and result, updated as the league's results secretary enters them. Your club's website usually mirrors its own teams' fixtures, and the group chat fills in anything else.

For a volunteer coach, the practical routine is simple: check Full-Time midweek for your division, confirm kickoff and venue with the opposition, then post one message to parents rather than ten.

Why fixtures change and how postponements are announced

Postponements come from weather, unplayable pitches and cup clashes, and they can land as late as Saturday morning. The league announces them through Full-Time and club secretaries, who then relay them to managers. If you rely on the group chat alone, you will hear it second-hand and late.

All three county youth leagues run on the same basic model: age groups from Under 7s upwards, fixtures organised by division, and results published online rather than on paper. The difference you notice as a coach is where those fixtures live and how quickly they appear.

Dorset football league youth: structure and fixtures

Dorset's youth setup follows the same county FA pattern: a league running age-group divisions with fixtures and results published through The FA's Full-Time system, the same tooling North Wiltshire uses. If you have managed in one county, the layout will feel familiar.

Oxfordshire youth football league fixtures: where to look

How do youth leagues like Berkshire, Mid Cheshire and Ayrshire publish fixtures and results?

County youth leagues run on different rhythms, and knowing the pattern saves you a Sunday-night scramble. Here is how three of them typically handle it.

Berkshire and Mid Cheshire: midweek and postponed fixtures

Both leagues lean on a central fixture list that covers every age group from Under 7s upwards. Midweek fixtures, common in the run-in when cup replays and rescheduled matches pile up, are added on top of the Sunday list rather than replacing it. Postponements are the weak point: the league site records the change, but parents relying on the group chat often hear it last. Checking the league page yourself, then relaying a short confirmation to parents, beats forwarding a screenshot.

Ayrshire: what Scottish youth leagues do differently

Scottish youth football sits under the SFA's banner, so Ayrshire fixtures follow a similar Sunday pattern but with a longer winter break and different age-group cutoffs. The publishing habit is the same though: one central list, updated when games move.

What should parents check before their child joins a league club?

Before signing anything, confirm the club actually exists in the league. Ask the manager for the club's FA affiliation number and check the team appears on the league's published fixture lists for the right age group. County youth leagues publish their structure and fixtures openly, so a genuine club is easy to spot.

Confirming the club is affiliated and insured

Affiliation with your county FA means the club holds public liability and personal accident insurance for registered players, and that coaches have completed DBS checks and safeguarding training. Ask to see the affiliation confirmation for the current season, not last year's. If the club cannot produce it, walk away.

Questions to ask the welfare officer

Every youth club must have a named welfare officer. Ask three things: is the officer's DBS and safeguarding qualification current, how are concerns reported, and what are the rules on photography and changing rooms? A good welfare officer answers without hesitation and puts you in touch with the county FA's safeguarding team if you want a second opinion.

Why are league websites slow to update, and what can you do about it?

League websites lag because a volunteer types the results in, and that volunteer has a job, kids and a Sunday night to contend with. Postponements travel the same way: someone at the club emails the fixtures secretary, who updates the site when they get a minute. The site is not broken; the pipeline behind it is one unpaid person.

The realistic workarounds for you as a coach:

  • Treat the league site as the record, not the feed. Check it on Monday, not Sunday evening.
  • Keep your own team's results and player of the match notes somewhere you control, so you are never waiting on the county site to remember the season.
  • Confirm postponements by phone or direct message with the opposing manager, not the website.

What does a matchday admin routine look like for a volunteer coach?

Sunday night, fifteen minutes, four steps.

First, check the league site for next week's fixture, kick-off time and venue, and note anything that has changed since the fixture list was published. Second, confirm your line-up and playing time so every child gets a fair share, and post it to the group chat in one message rather than a thread of replies.

Third, after the final whistle, share the score and player of the match while it is fresh. A first goal or a clean sheet deserves more than a buried WhatsApp message.

FAQ

Is the North Wiltshire Youth Football League part of the FA, and is it affiliated?

It is not run by the FA directly. The league is affiliated to the county FA and administered by its own volunteer committee, which means member clubs get sanctioned fixtures, insurance through affiliation and safeguarding oversight. The FA's Full-Time system publishes fixtures and results, but the committee itself is local and unpaid.

What age groups can play, from Under 7s to Under 11s and beyond?

The league covers youth age groups from Under 7s upwards, following the FA's youth football structure. The youngest ages play smaller-sided formats, building towards 11v11 as children get older, so an Under 8s side plays a different format from an Under 12s team. Your club secretary registers each team with the league for its correct age group.

Where can I find results if the league website hasn't updated since Saturday?

Check the FA's Full-Time system, which is the official record for every division, then your club's own channels. If the result still is not there, the results secretary has simply not entered it yet. Keeping your own team's results and player of the match notes somewhere you control means you never wait on the league to remember the season.

How do I know if a match is postponed because of the weather?

Postponements are announced through Full-Time and passed to club secretaries, who relay them to managers. They can land as late as Saturday morning, so check the division's Full-Time page yourself rather than relying on the group chat, where news arrives second-hand. Confirming directly with the opposing manager by phone or message is the reliable backup.
