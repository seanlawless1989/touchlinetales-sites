This guide turns the FA's scattered documents into one season-by-season checklist, showing exactly what a one-team volunteer coach must do versus what a multi-team club must do before the first whistle.

What are the new FA rules for grassroots football in 26/27?

From the 2026/27 season, the FA is rolling out changes to youth formats under its Future Fit programme: smaller-sided games, smaller pitches, and a phased rollout that starts with the youngest age groups and moves up over successive seasons. The aim is more touches of the ball per player, more goals, and a better first experience of the game for children in the early years.

Why the FA is changing youth formats

The FA's stated reason is player development: fewer children on the pitch means more involvement per child, which matters most at Under 6 to Under 8, where a first goal or a fair share of playing time shapes whether a child stays in the game. The changes sit alongside existing FA rules on safeguarding and welfare officers. The FA's own announcement sets out the detail at England Football.

Which age groups are affected first

The rollout starts with the youngest teams and phases up through the age groups in later seasons, so an Under 7s coach in 26/27 faces different prep than an Under 11s coach. Either way, your season record, line-ups and player-by-player milestones will need updating as formats shift, which is exactly what a team site like Touchline Tales keeps in one place.

How do new team sizes change junior grassroots football formats?

The FA's Future Fit plans move smaller formats up the age bands, so the jump between sizes happens earlier. The headline shift for most volunteer coaches: 9v9 football arrives at Under 11 rather than Under 12, while 5v5 and 7v7 stay in place below that.

Squad sizes and playing time

Smaller formats mean smaller squads and more touches per player, which is the point. If you carry a bigger squad for absences, rotating fair playing time gets harder, not easier. What do the Future Fit changes mean for your child's playing experience?

More touches, more goals, more decision-making

Smaller teams mean each child gets more of the ball. The FA's youth football format regulations set team sizes, pitch and goal dimensions by age group, so an Under 8 playing 5v5 on a smaller pitch takes more touches and more shots per match than they would in a bigger format. More touches build confidence; more goals build the habit of coming back. Fair playing time matters more than results at this age, and the new formats make it easier for a volunteer coach to rotate a squad without leaving anyone on the touchline all morning.

What parents should expect on matchday

Expect shorter pitches, smaller goals and a louder buzz from more children scoring. Your coach will be reworking line-ups and rotation plans around the new team sizes, and a simple team site keeps every match, player of the match and first goal on record through the season. Welfare officers should also check the safeguarding side of the transition with the club's welfare officer checklist.

What must grassroots football clubs do before the 26/27 season starts?

The Future Fit changes only bite if someone actually does the prep. Split the work by role, start early, and nothing on matchday looks different.

Checklist for a one-team volunteer coach

If you run one team, your list is short and physical. Confirm your new format and pitch size for the age group, then check your goals and pitch markings match it before the first friendly. Rebuild your line-up templates around the new team size, and put a note in the group chat in July telling parents what changes and why. If you keep a season record, set it up now so the new format is captured from game one.

Checklist for a multi-team club

The long paragraph worth quoting: a club secretary's job is coordination, and the failure mode is each coach solving the same problem differently. One Under 8s coach marks out the new pitch with cones, another borrows full-size goals from the shed, and by October the club has three interpretations of the same rule and a welfare officer fielding confused parents. Write one club-wide plan: which pitches get re-marked and when, which goals move where, who books the fixtures under the new formats, and what every parent receives, in the same wording, from every team. Circulate it in June, not August. Clubs that standardise once spend nothing extra during the season; clubs that leave it to individual coaches pay for it every Sunday in corrections and apologies.

Timeline: what to do each month before kickoff

June: confirm formats and order any new goals or markings. July: brief coaches and send the first parent note. August: mark pitches, run a friendly under the new rules, and finish your comms setup, whether that is your club site or a scheduling tool like Spond or Pitchero. September: first league fixture, nothing new to learn.

How does FA funding for grassroots football support the format changes?

The FA has tied Future Fit money to pitch and facility investment, with the bulk aimed at small-sided pitches that suit smaller team sizes at Under 7s to Under 11s. National Game Strategy detail, including rollout dates, sits on the FA's own site (The FA, National Game Strategy). Your County FA holds the local picture: which sites in your area are being rebuilt, and what your club can apply for.

Where the funding is going

Money targets pitch provision rather than kit or kit-adjacent extras: new small-sided surfaces, pitch regrading, and drainage so more matches survive the winter. Clubs with several junior sides, or sites shared between teams, tend to be first in line because one improved site serves more children.

How a club finds out if it qualifies

Contact your County FA's facilities lead and ask what is already earmarked for your area. Qualification is qualitative: demonstrated demand, a site agreement, and a club willing to maintain the surface. If your club is applying, a single record of every match and player helps evidence demand, and Touchline Tales' data processing agreement covers how that data is handled.

Which grants for grassroots football clubs can help cover the transition?

Football Foundation and county FA grants

The Football Foundation is the main route for pitch work, goalposts, and equipment. Smaller-format goals for Under 7s to Under 11s and pitch marking changes are exactly the kind of spend its facilities grants are designed for. Your county FA often runs smaller schemes on top, typically covering starter equipment like portable goals, bibs, and training kit. Ask your club secretary or welfare officer which local council funds exist too; many districts have small community grants for junior sport that go unclaimed.

Writing a simple grant application that works

Keep it short and specific: what you are buying, how many children it serves, and the FA format rules that make it necessary. A quote for 9v9 or smaller goals plus your player numbers is stronger than a page of background. County FA staff will often review a draft before you submit, so ask.

How do referees' roles change under the new grassroots football rules?

Modified laws for smaller formats

Future Fit shrinks teams and pitches from 26/27, and referees adapt to match: shorter games, rolling substitutions and simplified laws for 5v5 and 7v7. The FA's format guidance sets pitch and goal sizes per age group, so a referee overseeing Under 8s applies different expectations than one on an 11v11 pitch. Many small-sided fixtures at the youngest ages still rely on club volunteers or parents rather than qualified officials, so your club's referee supply matters as much as the rulebook.

Briefing your matchday helpers

Before the first fixture, walk your referee and parent helpers through the format: match length, retreat lines, and how throw-ins and restarts work at your age group. Point parents to the FA's respect guidance so sideline behaviour matches the modified laws. Funding for referee courses and pitch improvements sits with bodies like the Football Foundation, which part-finances grassroots facilities nationally, so ask your club secretary what local support exists.

How do you keep parents informed through the transition?

One announcement beats twenty WhatsApp messages

Format changes are coming, and parents will ask the same questions repeatedly. Post one written announcement with the new team sizes, pitch sizes and kickoff times, then point every reply back to it. A single team site holding fixtures, line-ups and match records means nobody scrolls through months of group chat to find the Under 9s format. Keep the group chat for quick matchday logistics; keep the season record somewhere permanent.

FAQ

Do the new grassroots football rules apply to girls' teams at the same time?

Yes. Future Fit changes apply to boys' and girls' teams on the same phased schedule, starting with the youngest age groups in 26/27. The format for an Under 7s side is the same regardless of gender, so a girls' team coach prepares the same pitch, goal and team-size checks as any other coach before the season starts.

What happens if our pitch is too big for the new format in 26/27?

You have options. Temporary cone markings work for friendlies and many small-sided fixtures, and portable goals can replace full-size ones at the youngest ages. For permanent re-marking, pitch work is exactly what FA funding and Football Foundation grants target. Contact your County FA's facilities lead early, since regrading and remarking queues build up as clubs transition at once.

Can my child still play up an age group under the new team sizes?

Playing up remains possible, but the format your child's team plays is set by the age group they are registered in, not by their age alone. Because team sizes now differ between age groups, a child playing up joins a different format with different pitch and goal dimensions. Confirm with your league and club secretary before committing, since approval rules vary.

Do I need new goals and kit, and roughly what will that cost?

Smaller formats call for smaller goals, and costs vary by size, material and whether you buy portable or socketed goals, so get quotes rather than trusting a single figure. Pitch marking changes cost little if you do them yourself with cones or line paint. Funding routes exist: Football Foundation facilities grants and county FA starter schemes both cover goals and equipment.

Will results and league tables still count for junior grassroots football under the new formats?

It depends on age group. At the youngest ages, many leagues already emphasise development over results, and smaller formats reinforce that. Older junior age groups generally keep competitive fixtures and tables under the new team sizes. Your league sets what applies to your division, so ask your fixtures secretary what changes for your age group in 26/27.
