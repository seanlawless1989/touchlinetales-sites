If you are asking how many players on youth football team rosters are allowed in England, the answer depends on age: Under-7s and Under-8s play 5v5, Under-9s and Under-10s play 7v7, Under-11s and Under-12s play 9v9, and from Under-13s up it is 11v11. FA maximum squad sizes rise from 5 at the youngest ages to 16 at the oldest.

How many players are allowed on a youth football team by age group?

The FA caps squad sizes by age group, and the cap rises as the pitch gets bigger: 5 for Under 7s and Under 8s, 10 for Under 9s and Under 10s, 14 for Under 11s and Under 12s, and 16 from Under 13s up to Under 18s. Each cap pairs with a match format: 5v5 (mini-soccer) for the youngest, 7v7 at U9–U10, 9v9 at U11–U12, and 11v11 from U13 onwards, per The FA's youth football guidance.

What is the maximum squad size for under-7s to under-10s?

Under-7s and Under-8s play 5v5, and the FA caps squads at a maximum of 8 players per team. Under-9s and Under-10s move to 7v7, with a maximum squad size of 12. These are the mini-soccer rules that apply across English grassroots football from Under 7s to Under 11s, so the jump to 9v9 comes later.

Why mini-soccer caps squads tighter than the FA maximum

In practice, most leagues set their own roster limits below those FA maximums, and some cap 5v5 squads at 7 and 7v7 squads at 10. The reason is simple: a squad of 12 at Under 9 means four children sit out much of every match, which makes fair playing time harder for a volunteer coach to guarantee. A tighter roster keeps more kids on the pitch for longer, and fewer parents asking why their child only got five minutes.

The trade-off is a thinner squad on days when illness and birthday parties hit. Whatever number your league settles on, keep a running note of who played which weeks and share results with parents so nobody has to reconstruct the season from WhatsApp messages.

When do youth teams move from 7v7 to 9v9 and 11v11?

Mini-soccer covers the early years: 5v5 at Under 7s and Under 8s, 7v7 at Under 9s and Under 10s, with squad caps to match, as set out in the FA's mini-soccer laws and handbook. The first jump comes at Under 11 and Under 12, where teams move to 9v9 on a larger pitch. From Under 13 upwards it is the full 11-a-side game.

Each step up adds players to the pitch and to your registration list. A 7v7 squad that felt comfortable at Under 10 needs two or three more signed-on players to field a 9v9 team with substitutes, and roughly five more again for 11v11 at Under 13.

What the jump to 11v11 means for your squad size

Eleven-a-side is where squad depth starts to matter week to week. School holidays, illness and cup fixtures mean 14 to 16 registered players is a realistic minimum if you want to put out a full team with subs every Saturday. How many players should you actually register versus the FA maximum?

Registering right up to the FA maximum sounds safe, but it usually backfires. If you carry 16 players at Under 9s, where the format is 7v7, you will struggle to give everyone fair playing time, and the parents who watch their child sit out most Sundays will let you hear about it. A smaller squad means every player gets real minutes, and you spend less time on Sunday night juggling line-ups.

The honest rule of thumb is to register for the team you actually field, not the team you might field. Start by working out your realistic matchday attendance rather than your signup list, because holidays, birthday parties and illness reliably take a quarter to a third of any youth squad out on a given weekend. A squad that turns out nine-strong on paper often fields six on the day. Add one or two spares beyond your realistic attendance number, and stop there.

Over-registering feels like insurance, but it converts directly into children standing on the touchline in the drizzle, which is the one outcome fair playing time rules exist to prevent. If you keep a written record of who played each week, the pattern becomes obvious within a month and you can adjust your registered numbers at the next window.

Worked example: a 14-player squad at Under 9s on matchday

Say you register 14 players for a 7v7 Under 9s team. On a typical Sunday expect three or four absent, leaving ten or eleven available. With rolling subs, that gives everyone meaningful minutes across a 20-minute-each-way match, and no child plays fewer than half the game. If attendance runs high, rotate starts week to week and keep a written note of minutes so your decisions survive the car park conversation. If you had registered 16 instead, two more children would share the same minutes, and the maths stops working.

What happens if your squad exceeds the FA limit?

Nothing dramatic at first, because the FA sets no maximum squad size for most youth age groups. The limits that bite come from your league, which caps how many players you can register per team, and from matchday rules like squad lists at 9v9 and 11v11. Register a 17th player at Under 11 and the league will usually refuse the registration or fine the club.

The practical fix is splitting. If you have 18 Under 11s, run two 9-player squads: two 9v9 teams, two Sunday fixtures, two sets of line-ups. The FA's youth guidance sets the format, 9v9 up to Under 12 and 11v11 from Under 13, so two smaller teams keep you inside both the format and fair playing time.

The honest catch: splitting doubles your volunteer workload, and most leagues require a second registered coach. If you cannot staff two teams, cap your registrations at what one matchday can absorb and run a waiting list instead.

How do substitutions and rolling subs work in youth football?

Youth football in England uses rolling substitutions: once a player has been named in the squad, they can come off and go back on as many times as needed, with the referee's okay, while play continues. That is why FA formats carry a generous bench. Under 7s to Under 10s can name up to five substitutes, Under 11s and Under 12s up to three, and older youth teams typically five, so every registered child gets match time rather than a seat.

Rolling subs are a blessing and a trap. Swapping every ten minutes is easy to promise and hard to track when you are also managing a game. The FA's expectation is fair playing time, and a parent-coach who forgets which child sat out the last two fixtures will hear about it.

Keeping track of playing time without a spreadsheet

A notes app works at Under 7s. By Under 10s, with three subs rotating, most coaches want the substitutions written down per half. How do squad size rules differ between mini-soccer and youth football?

The FA splits youth football into three tiers. Mini-soccer covers Under 7s to Under 10s at 5v5 or 7v7, with the smallest pitches and goals. From Under 11 to Under 12, teams move to 9v9 on larger pitches with bigger goals. From Under 13 upwards, it is full 11v11 on adult-sized pitches.

Substitution rules shift with the format. In all three tiers, rolling substitutions are allowed, so players can return to the pitch after coming off, as set out in the FA's youth football guidance.

Practically, that means a 7v7 squad of nine or ten rotates freely, while an 11v11 squad of sixteen still expects every registered player to get meaningful minutes each week.

FAQ

Can my child play up an age group if the squad is short?

Usually yes, but it needs sign-off. The FA allows playing up one age group with parental consent and your league's or County FA's approval, and leagues typically handle short-squad situations this way rather than forcing a forfeit. Ask your league secretary before matchday, since approval processes differ and some leagues restrict how often a player can play up.

How many subs can you use in a youth football match?

Rolling subs apply at every age group, so named substitutes can go on and off repeatedly with the referee's permission. The FA's guidance allows up to five substitutes at Under 7s to Under 10s, up to three at Under 11s and Under 12s, and typically five for older age groups. Every registered player should expect actual minutes, not a seat on the bench.

Do girls' youth teams have the same squad size rules?

Broadly yes. The FA sets the same age-group formats for girls and boys: 5v5 and 7v7 in mini-soccer up to Under 10s, 9v9 at Under 11s and Under 12s, and 11v11 from Under 13s. Girls' leagues may run slight variations, such as delayed transitions to 11v11 in some counties, so check your specific league's handbook for its registered squad caps.

What is the smallest squad you can field without forfeiting?

It depends on the format and your league. Most leagues let a team start short, so a 9v9 side might kick off with seven and an 11v11 side with nine, rather than conceding the fixture. Below the format's minimum, the match is usually awarded to the opposition. Confirm your league's exact threshold, because a few require the full number to start.
