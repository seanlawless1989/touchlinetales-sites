Joining the Swindon and District Sunday Football League means registering your team by the league's pre-season deadline, paying entry and registration fees, then handling a weekly rhythm of fixture checks, result reporting, discipline paperwork and postponement calls. This guide walks through each step in order, with the deadlines and match secretary duties most league pages leave out, so your first season runs on a checklist rather than guesswork.

How do you register a new team with the Swindon and District Sunday Football League?

You enter a team by contacting the league secretary before the close-season deadline, paying the annual entry fee, and completing affiliation through England Football so the club gets an FA team number. Most new managers do this in June or July, and the league's own site and county FA pages set out the paperwork. Start with the league and county FA guidance on affiliation, because your players cannot kick a ball in a fixture until each one is registered to your club.

What paperwork and player registrations are needed

Expect a club application, a list of registered players with dates of birth, photos for ID cards in youth age groups, and confirmation that you have a welfare officer if you run Under 7s to Under 11s. Each player is registered individually on the FA system, and you add or de-register mid-season as the squad changes.

Key deadlines in the close season

Team entry and fees are usually due in early summer, with player registrations finalised before the first fixture in September. Miss the entry window and you wait a full year, so diary it the moment the season ends. Once you are in, keeping the squad list and match details in one place, such as a Touchline Tales team site, saves you retyping line-ups every Sunday night.

What does it cost to play in a Sunday league in Swindon?

A single team's season budget has four parts: the league's annual fee, county FA affiliation, match officials, and small running costs. The league sets its own annual fee per team and invoices it before the season starts; ask the league secretary for the current figure, since it changes year to year. Affiliation is paid to the county FA and covers insurance and eligibility to play.

Who pays what: club, team or parents

In most grassroots setups the club pays affiliation and league fees from its own account, then either charges parents a signing-on fee or collects match subs weekly. If you are a standalone team, you carry all of it yourself. Agree the split before pre-season, not after the first invoice.

When are fixtures published and how are postponements handled?

The Swindon and District Sunday Football League releases a full season fixture list before the opening Sunday, then publishes weekly fixtures on its league website and the FA Full-Time system. Kickoff is usually Sunday morning, and the match secretary confirms details with the opposition by midweek.

The weekly fixture rhythm

Expect a Sunday-night pattern: check the published fixture, confirm the venue and kickoff, then send the line-up to your group chat. Keeping a season record in one place, such as a team site like Touchline Tales, means you are not scrolling WhatsApp for last week's team.

Postponement and rearrangement protocol

Waterlogged pitches and frozen grounds are the main cause of Sunday cancellations. The home club notifies the league and the away club as early as possible, and the fixture is rearranged through the fixture secretary. Unplayed fixtures must be replayed, and failure to fulfil them can bring fines under county discipline rules — see the Wiltshire County FA guidance for teams.

What do match secretaries need to do each week?

A Sunday-night checklist

Once the season starts, the match secretary's week settles into a rhythm: check the league's published fixtures midweek, confirm pitch booking and kick-off time with the opposition, text the squad, then fill in the team sheet before the first whistle. After the match, you have a short window to get the result to the league, usually by text, email or the league's results portal, and any player of the match or goalscorer details if the division records them. Miss the deadline and you're chasing the fixtures secretary by phone on Monday morning.

That Sunday-night hour is where most of the season's admin actually lives, and it is the same handful of tasks every single week: confirm the fixture is still on, check postponements haven't been announced, send the meet time to the group chat, print or copy the team sheet, then report the result before the league's cutoff. None of it is hard. All of it is easy to forget when you've just driven home from an away fixture in the dark. Writing the checklist down once, and reusing it, is what keeps a volunteer coach sane across a full season.

Discipline and reporting in district Sunday leagues

If a player is booked or sent off, the referee's report goes to the county FA, but the club is expected to log the incident and pay any fine promptly. Keep your own note of the match, the player and the offence so you can respond if the discipline paperwork arrives days later.

How does discipline and reporting work in district Sunday leagues?

Discipline in the Swindon and District Sunday Football League runs through the Wiltshire County FA, not through the league itself. Referees report cautions and sendings-off on their match paperwork, and the county FA issues the charge to your club. As team secretary you are expected to respond to any charge within the deadline on the paperwork, usually within a week of it arriving.

What happens after a red card

A sending-off triggers an automatic suspension, typically starting the following Sunday. For youth and open-age teams, the county FA handles the case under FA disciplinary rules, and clubs working with Under-18 players must also keep safeguarding records current, including welfare officer details and DBS checks for coaches (The FA's guidance on safeguarding and registration requirements). Keep a note of the referee's match report reference so you can track the case.

Fines and appeals

Fines are set by the county FA and vary by offence; treat published figures from other leagues as illustrative only. You can appeal any charge, but appeals cost money and rarely succeed unless there is a clear procedural error. Pay, serve the ban, and move on.

How are results and standings shared with players and parents?

What the league publishes and when

The league handles the official record. Full-time results go to the league's results secretary, who publishes them and updates the division table, usually on the league's website or its fixture platform within a day or two of Sunday's games. That is the version that counts for promotion, relegation and top-scorer claims. It covers the whole division, not your squad, and it never includes your player of the match or the Under 9s' first goal.

Sharing results without WhatsApp chaos

Parents do not want the league table. They want to know whether their child played, what the score was, and who took the match award. Most teams end up posting all of that into the group chat alongside lift requests and "can anyone sub?" messages, and it drowns by Tuesday.

A team site fixes the split: the league keeps the official standings, and your site keeps the season, match by match, in your crest and colours. One match report post covers everyone, so the chat goes back to logistics. A tool like Spond or Pitchero can do the same job at team level; Touchline Tales is built around that season-long record, child by child, rather than the weekly blast.

How does the Swindon league compare to nearby ones like Ashford or Burton and District?

What is common to all district Sunday leagues

District Sunday leagues run on the same FA framework: affiliate with the county FA, register your squad before a deadline, pay team and player fees, and file results through the league's chosen system, usually the FA's Full-Time. Match secretaries have weekly duties everywhere, and discipline flows through county FA reporting regardless of which league you play in.

Where leagues differ

The differences are practical. Fixture publication dates, postponement notice periods, matchday kick-off times, and how results must be reported all vary league by league. The Ashford District Sunday Football League's fixtures and registration details on FA Full-Time show the same structure Swindon uses, but check each league's own handbook for deadlines and fines before committing, since these are set locally and change season to season.

What should a first-season team expect week to week?

Common first-season surprises

Expect pitch availability to be your first headache: county FA pitch bookings and council grounds mean kick-off times can shift at short notice. Referee no-shows happen, and FA rules let a club volunteer officiate instead, so know who on your touchline is willing. You will also get postponements from weather and a few fixtures rearranged midweek, so keep the group chat flexible rather than fixed.

The bigger surprise is playing time. Parents judge your season on fair playing time, not results, so rotate deliberately from Under 7s to Under 11s and say so in advance. Plan who covers matchday when you cannot.

Keeping a record of the season

Without a record, the season vanishes into WhatsApp messages. Keep one place for line-ups, results, player of the match and first goals, child by child. Our own Touchline Tales does exactly this in your club crest and colours, though it will not fix pitch bookings or referees for you.

FAQ

Can my child play Sunday league football alongside Saturday junior football?

Yes, and many do. Saturday junior football and Sunday league are separate competitions, so your child registers with each club individually and fixtures rarely clash, since Sunday kickoffs are usually morning slots. Check both clubs accept the dual commitment, and make sure the Sunday team has the child's registration details, including date of birth and any ID requirements for youth age groups.

How many players do you need to register before the league accepts a team?

The league sets a minimum squad size in its handbook, and it varies by age group, so confirm the figure with the league secretary before paying the entry fee. In practice, register more than the bare minimum: with postponements, school holidays and no-shows across roughly 20 fixtures, a thin squad leaves you unable to fulfil fixtures, which can bring fines under county discipline rules.

Who reports the result if the referee fails to turn up?

Referee no-shows happen, and FA rules let a club volunteer officiate instead, so agree before kickoff who on your touchline is willing. The result reporting duty stays with the clubs as normal: the home side sends the score to the league within the usual window, and the volunteer referee still files any discipline paperwork with the county FA.

Do Sunday league clubs need a welfare officer even for open-age teams?

The formal welfare officer requirement applies to youth football, particularly Under 7s to Under 11s, where safeguarding records, welfare officer details and DBS checks for coaches must stay current with the county FA. For open-age teams it is not a registration condition, but if your club runs any youth sides alongside the adult team, the welfare officer covers the whole club.

What happens if we cannot raise a team one Sunday?

You cannot simply skip it. The home club notifies the league and the away club as early as possible, and the fixture is rearranged through the fixture secretary. Unplayed fixtures must be replayed, and failure to fulfil them can bring fines under county discipline rules, so contact the league before the deadline. Rearranged games usually land midweek, so keep the group chat flexible.
