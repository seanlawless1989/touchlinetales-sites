The West Lothian youth football league runs youth football across West Lothian, Scotland, from age-group divisions through a season of weekly fixtures. This guide covers the format, then the week-to-week workflows: where fixtures appear, what happens when a pitch is called off, and how results reach parents.

What is the West Lothian youth football league and who plays in it?

The West Lothian youth football league is a Scottish Youth Football Association-affiliated league serving clubs across West Lothian, from Livingston and Bathgate to Broxburn and Linlithgow. It runs organised youth football for age groups from the youngest four-a-side formats through to Under 18s and, in some cases, 19s, giving local volunteer coaches a structured fixture list for the whole season.

For a parent-coach running one team, the league is the framework you work inside: it sets your fixtures, your age-group rules and your matchday calendar, while your club handles registration and welfare.

Where the league sits alongside other Scottish youth leagues

Like other SYFA-affiliated regional leagues, it operates under Scottish FA youth football rules rather than inventing its own (Scottish FA). How clubs share weekly results with families is covered separately in our guide to sharing results with parents.

What age groups and divisions does the West Lothian youth football league run?

The league runs age bands from the youngest sections through to youth football, with the format of play changing as children get older. Younger squads play small-sided games on smaller pitches, and the pitch, ball and goal size grow with each step up until teams reach the full 11-a-side game.

Format of play by age band

The exact band boundaries can shift season to season, so confirm with the league or your club secretary before planning your year. The broad pattern looks like this:

Age bandTypical formatWhat changes for coaches
Under 6 to Under 74v4, no published resultsFocus on touches, not tactics
Under 8 to Under 117v7First line-ups, rotating goalkeeper
Under 12 and up9v9, then 11v11Full pitch, set positions, results matter

At the small-sided ages, FA-aligned child-centred rules apply: retreat lines, roll-on roll-off subs and an emphasis on fair playing time over winning.

How divisions and seeding work

As squads move into competitive football, teams are grouped into divisions by ability rather than age alone, usually after early-season results or seeding games. That means your Sunday opponent is typically a side at a similar level, which is worth remembering when a 9-0 defeat lands in the group chat.

If you are a parent-coach stepping into one of these age bands for the first time, our guide for volunteer coaches covers the week-to-week basics.

How does the season and fixture calendar work?

The West Lothian youth football season runs broadly with the school year, kicking off in August and finishing around late spring, with most matches played on a Sunday. Fixture lists are built around age groups, with younger teams playing small-sided formats under Scottish Youth Football Association rules before moving to eleven-a-side.

Winter break and weather

Frost and waterlogged pitches mean the published fixture list is a plan, not a promise. Postponements get decided late, often on a matchday morning, and the news travels through the group chat in a scramble. That is why the club secretary's job of rebuilding the calendar matters as much as the original list, and why a team site that logs every rearranged date beats scrolling WhatsApp messages. For volunteers balancing those comms duties, the welfare officer checklist covers the season-long admin in one place.

How are fixtures published and where can parents find weekly match times?

League channels vs club channels

The league publishes the season's fixture list centrally, but kick-off times and pitches are usually confirmed club by club, later in the week. So the league list tells you who you play; your own club's channel tells you where and when.

A 5-minute Sunday-night routine for coaches

On Sunday evening, check the league fixtures for next week's opponent, then message your parents' group chat with the confirmed kick-off time, venue and what time players should arrive. Pin that message so it stays at the top all week. Ask parents to reply with a yes or no per child, so you can count heads before Wednesday training. If anything changes, a postponement, a pitch switch, send one fresh message rather than burying the update in a thread. Doing this once a week, at the same time, means families know exactly when to look, and you stop answering the same "what time on Saturday?" text ten times over, because the answer is already pinned where everyone can find it, and new parents joining mid-season can scroll back to one message instead of asking the chat to repeat itself.

What happens when a match is postponed or cancelled at short notice?

Pitch inspections and call-off timelines

The call usually starts with the home club or the fixture secretary, who inspects the pitch or reacts to a council closure, often on the Friday evening or early Saturday morning. The secretary then notifies the away club's manager, who is responsible for passing the message on. Weather can also force a same-morning call-off, so treat any decision before kick-off as live until you are standing on the pitch.

A cancellation message template that works

When the call comes, send one message to the whole group chat rather than a drip of replies. Cover the essentials: which match is off, who made the call, whether training replaces it, and when the next update will land. Something like: "Under 9s v X, Saturday 10am, OFF, pitch waterlogged, decision by home club. Training as normal Saturday 9am. Next update Friday." Pin it so nobody scrolls past it.

How do clubs in the league share results and updates with families?

Most teams run on the same Sunday-night ritual: the coach posts the score, a few scorers, and a player of the match straight into the group chat, usually from the car park. It works until it doesn't, because the chat holds everything and remembers nothing.

Why the group chat struggles as the system of record

A WhatsApp thread mixes fixture changes, kit reminders and a photo of the dog, so the one result a grandparent wants is buried under forty messages by Tuesday. Nobody can answer "did we win away at X in October?" without scrolling. There's also a safeguarding angle: coaches handling children's information should be PVG-checked under Scottish FA wellbeing and protection requirements, which is a reason to keep comms structured rather than sprawled across personal accounts.

A team site fixes the record: every result, scorer and first goal stays findable all season, in the club's crest and colours. The chat can go back to being a chat.

How can new players register with a club in the league?

Most families start by finding the club nearest to them. Clubs in West Lothian usually advertise via social media, school newsletters or word of mouth at the local pitch, so a quick search for your child's age group plus your town is a reasonable first step. Many clubs run specific sign-up days before the season starts.

Once you've picked a club, registration typically means filling in the club's own form with your child's details, a photo, and emergency contacts, plus paying a signing-on fee. The club then submits your child's registration to the league through its secretary, who handles the paperwork with the SYFA.

Ask the club when registration closes. Places at younger age groups fill quickly, and a player can't play in league fixtures until the registration is confirmed.

What roles do volunteers and welfare officers play in running league teams?

A grassroots team typically runs on three volunteer roles. The coach or manager picks line-ups, runs training and sends matchday details to the group chat. The club secretary handles registration of players, fixture confirmations with the league and reporting results. The welfare officer is the safeguarding point of contact for parents and players, and every club with youth teams must have one in place.

Each role carries checks. Coaches and welfare officers complete safeguarding training and disclosure checks before working with children, and the club keeps records of who is cleared for which duty.

PVG checks and safeguarding basics in Scotland

In Scotland, volunteers in regulated work with children join the Protecting Vulnerable Groups (PVG) scheme, which the club arranges through its association. You can read how the scheme works on the Scottish Government's PVG guidance pages. Week to week, the practical split is simple: the coach communicates, the secretary records, the welfare officer safeguards.

How does the league compare in structure to other youth leagues like Wigan's?

West Lothian and an English equivalent such as the Wigan youth league follow the same broad shape: age bands from the youngest kicks through to open age, a season running autumn to spring, and weekly fixtures published by the league rather than arranged club to club. The differences are mostly administrative. Scottish youth football sits under the Scottish FA's pathway and its own rules on formats and playing time, while a Wigan team answers to the English FA's versions of the same. For a relocating coach, the week-to-week work, the group chat, the line-ups, the postponements, transfers almost unchanged.

FAQ

What age are the youngest players in the West Lothian youth football league?

The youngest age bands start around Under 6 and Under 7, playing 4v4 with no published results. Formats then build up through 7v7 and 9v9 before teams reach the full 11-a-side game. Exact band boundaries can shift from season to season, so confirm the current structure with the league or your club secretary before planning your year.

What day do West Lothian youth football league matches kick off?

Most matches are played on a Sunday, with the season running from August through to late spring alongside the school year. Kick-off times and pitches are confirmed club by club later in the week, so treat the league's published list as the opponent and your own club's channel as the source for the actual time and venue.

How do I find my child's team's fixtures each week?

Check the league's central fixture list for the opponent, then rely on your club's channel for the confirmed kick-off time and venue, usually shared in the team's group chat. Coaches should post one pinned message per week with the time, pitch and arrival time, and send a fresh message if a postponement or pitch switch happens.

Who do I contact if I have a safeguarding concern at a match?

Contact your club's welfare officer, who is the designated safeguarding point of contact for parents and players, and every club with youth teams must have one in place. Coaches and welfare officers complete safeguarding training and disclosure checks, including joining the PVG scheme, before working with children, so the welfare officer is the right first stop.
